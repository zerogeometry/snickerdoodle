# Low/no-cost, high-impact marketing channels — Snickerdoodle

> Brief, 2026-10-12. Options for Simon to choose from; not decided. Scored for a solo founder on a
> tight budget. Every channel respects the non-negotiables: **no child's data or image is ever
> used in marketing without the parent's explicit consent, nothing about a child is public, and
> the parent stays the controller.**

## The one constraint that shapes the timing

**No live orders until the six compliance gates close** (DPIA, Children's-Code assessment,
transfers, trust pages, ICO + DPAs, AI terms). That makes Christmas 2026 tight. So the plan is:
**build demand and partnerships now (waitlist and "founding families"), then switch on sales the
day the gates close.** Every channel below works as a waitlist driver first and a sales driver after.

### 1. School and nursery PTA fundraiser
**What:** the PTA or nursery runs a "class art show". Children draw at school or at home; each
**parent** gets a class code and makes their own child's keepsake (card, mug, print), and the
PTA earns, for example, 15% of every order. School Christmas-card fundraisers are already a
familiar UK tradition, so the ask is easy.

**Privacy design (important):** the school **never uploads children's art in bulk**. The parent
uploads at home, so the parent stays the controller and no child data flows through the school.
The class code is only an attribution tag on the order.

**Seasons:** Christmas cards (Oct–Nov), Mother's/Father's Day, and **end-of-year teacher gifts**
in July (a class drawing on a mug or tea towel; very strong).

**Needs building:** attributed "class codes", a PTA earnings report, and a one-page PTA pack
(letter home with a QR code).

### 2. The grandparent gift-link loop (already built)
**What:** after a parent's first creation, prompt "send this to the grandparents". The
pre-filled gift link with "join the family · 5% off" is already working end to end, and now
ticks items off the list. It's the cheapest acquisition there is: relatives arrive pre-sold, on a
phone, with the art already on the product.

**Small tweaks:** a "send to grandparents" step on the order confirmation page, and a gift-list
nudge before each "coming up" birthday (the family calendar already powers this).

### 3. Micro-influencer affiliate codes (your idea; recommended, with guardrails)
**Who:** UK parent creators with 5k–50k followers (micro audiences convert better and charge
nothing up front), plus craft/kids'-art accounts and nursery/teacher accounts.

**Deal:** gift them one keepsake from their own child's drawing, plus a **unique code**: their
viewers get, say, 10% off, and the creator earns, say, 10–15% of each sale. You pay only when it
sells.

**Guardrails:**
- Content must be labelled **#ad** (ASA/CAP code and CMA rules on influencer marketing).
- Encourage **art-and-product reveals, not children's faces**. This fits the brand and protects their kids.
- No code stacking with the join saving unless you decide it should stack.

**Needs building:** promo/affiliate codes, **priced server-side** (like the 5% saving, never
trusted from the client), attribution per code, and a payout ledger. This is a small, contained
build on the existing pricing engine; it needs tests in the same PR (non-negotiable for pricing).

### 4. Where mums are physically present
School Christmas fairs (Nov–Dec), summer fêtes, NCT and toddler groups, library rhyme-times,
soft-play cafés.

**Format:** a "draw something" table with crayons. A parent snaps the drawing on their phone,
sees it on a mug in seconds (no account needed), and gets a "founding families" offer by QR code.

**Cost:** a stall is often free or £10–£30, and a few sample products double as props.

**Privacy:** the parent does it on *their own* phone; we don't photograph children or take
artwork away.

### 5. Parent-to-parent referral
"Give a friend £5 off their first keepsake, get £5 credit." It only costs money when it works.

**Needs:** a referral code per account and server-side credit. It's the same machinery as #3,
so build it once.

### 6. Seasonal short-form video + Pinterest
Repeatable formats:
- "fridge door → forever" (drawing then product reveal)
- "what Grandma got"
- "the wonky bits, framed in gold"

Seasonal hooks: Christmas, Valentine's, Mother's Day (UK, March), Father's Day (June),
grandparents, back to school.

Pinterest boards for "gifts from kids' drawings" and "grandparent gifts". Pins live for months,
unlike posts.

### 7. Local press and the founder story
"Local parent builds a private home for children's art." Pitch local papers, parenting
newsletters and school newsletters. Pair it with #1 ("the school that turned its art show into a
fundraiser").

## Suggested 90-day sequence
1. **Now:** waitlist "founding families" landing (PECR-consented email only), founder story (#7),
   start recruiting 2–3 pilot PTAs or nurseries (#1) and 10 micro-creators (#3).
2. **Build (small, tested):** affiliate/referral/class codes on the server-side pricing engine,
   plus attribution reporting.
3. **Gates closed → go live:** switch on the PTA pilots and creator codes together. Prompt the
   gift-link loop (#2) in every confirmation.
4. **Measure:** cost per first order by channel (code attribution), gift-link open → purchase
   rate, % of buyers who join a family.

## What not to do (protects the brand and the non-negotiables)
- No paid behavioural ads targeting children, and no profiling of children. Ad targeting must
  stay on adults.
- No marketing using a real child's drawing or name without the parent's explicit, logged
  consent (P2's consented gallery is the right home for that).
- No "social feed" growth hacks. The Keepsake Home stays private.
