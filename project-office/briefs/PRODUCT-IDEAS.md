# Snickerdoodle — Branded Product Ideas

> **Cowork brief, 2026-06-26.** Ideas for own-brand products that ride the core engine (family art → keepsakes), with a lean into Simon's "preset-but-personal" idea: designs that are pre-composed *for a family* yet still customised (likenesses, names, colours). Scored to compare, not to decide — product/range calls are Simon's.

## How to read the scores (1–5)
- **Orig** — originality vs what families can already buy elsewhere. Higher = fresher.
- **Cost** — build + production + ops cost/complexity for a solo operator. **Higher = more expensive/risky to run** (so for desirability, lower is better).
- **Blue** — blue-ocean-ness: uncontested space. Higher = less crowded.
- **Win** — realistic likelihood of commercial success at our stage.
- **Role** — its job in the unit economics (per the roadmap): **Wedge** (frequency/data), **Margin** (cash engine), **AOV** (basket-lifter), **Retention** (reminder loop).

A quick note on the spine idea first, because four products hang off it 👇

## The unlock: "The Family Cast" (a capability, not a single SKU)
Render each family member — and pets — **once**, as a consistent stylised illustrated set in a chosen art style, then reuse that cast across many products (the literal "one input → 40+ products" promise, but for *people*). It's the differentiator: most print-on-demand does *one image on one product*; a coherent, reusable multi-subject family cast is harder to do and rare to find. It's also **IP-clean** (the family's own likeness, not licensed characters) and **margin-friendly** (the expensive generative render happens once, then rides cheap onto many items). Strategically this is the thing I'd protect and build toward; the products below marked 🎭 depend on it.

## Standouts (if I had to back five)
1. **The Family Cast system (#1 first)** — the real differentiator and moat; IP-clean, margin-friendly, and the source of #2/#3/#4/#14. Lead the brand identity with it.
2. **Year in Doodles annual book (#6)** — most on-brand, recurring, high-AOV, lowest novelty risk. Pairs with the flagship-book plan already in the roadmap.
3. **Grandparent gift bundle (#11)** — highest near-term win for least effort; it's the gift loop in a box.
4. **Stylised pet portrait (#14)** — broad, emotional, easy add to the cast; widens the audience cheaply.
5. **Acrylic fridge masterpiece (#8)** — cheap, high-delight first upsell off any saved drawing.

Keep **drawing-on-a-gift (#5)** running underneath as the dependable margin workhorse — just don't let it *define* the brand, since it's the most commoditised thing here.

## Honest risks / watch-outs
- **The cast's cost lives in consistency.** Rendering a recognisable, *consistent* multi-person+pet set is the hard generative problem — it's where compute and QC cost concentrate. Render once, cache the cast, reuse everywhere (and it's a Studio Plus-shaped capability, per D9). A weak likeness is worse than none in the family category, so it needs a human-pleasing QC gate.
- **Apparel (#2) is the cost trap** — sizing, returns and COGS make jumpers/pyjamas the riskiest margin despite the virality. Treat as a seasonal, pre-order-friendly experiment, not a core line.
- **Sequencing:** prove the commodity margin engine (#5) + the gift bundle (#11) first (cheap, fast), then launch the Family Cast as the signature brand moment, then the annual book as the retention flywheel. That order matches the roadmap's wedge→margin→AOV→retention logic.
- **Validate every COGS with real Prodigi/book-printer quotes** before pricing — the roadmap flags the unit-economics numbers as assumptions to test.

*Scores are my strategic judgement for comparison, not market research; worth pressure-testing against real competitor scans and POD quotes before committing range.*
