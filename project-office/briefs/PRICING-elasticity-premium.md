# Price elasticity & premium positioning — evidence pack

> Prepared 2026-07-03, prompted by the founder's question: what is the price elasticity of demand
> for personalised children's keepsake products, given parents' willingness to spend?
> Method: 6-angle web research sweep, every key numeric claim adversarially re-verified against
> the live source page. Companion to [CONTRADO-own-label-garments.md](CONTRADO-own-label-garments.md).

## 0. The one-paragraph answer
No credible published elasticity estimate exists for this niche (the oft-quoted "greeting cards
elasticity = −2, Hallmark econometricians" traces to a textbook homework exercise, not a study).
But the assembled evidence says demand for products bearing a child's OWN drawing behaves
**near-inelastically across a wide band (2–6× commodity POD prices are realised at volume)** —
*provided* the category has no strong cheap reference anchor, and *provided* the premium is
carried by a story (own art + organic/craft + keepsake permanence), not by the print alone.
Elasticity is category-specific: cards and mugs are anchored and elastic; storybooks, boxes,
jewellery-grade keepsakes and framed art are unanchored and price-tolerant.

## 1. Why this demand is structurally price-tolerant (mechanisms, with evidence)
- **No substitutes.** The subject matter is literally one-of-a-kind — the textbook first
  condition for inelastic demand. Revealed-preference auctions (real money, not surveys):
  Schreier 2006, n=185 Vickrey auctions — WTP for self-designed products +134% on average
  (+113% for T-shirts); Franke & Piller 2004 (watches) ~+100%. A child's own drawing is the
  extreme case of the self-design effect. Caveat from the same paper: ~12% of people derive
  NO premium value — a standard tier must remain.
- **Buyer ≠ recipient.** Flynn & Adams 2009: gift GIVERS believe more expensive = more
  thoughtful; recipients don't. The price-decision-maker is intrinsically price-tolerant.
  Camerer 1988: effortful, "inefficient" gifts are better signals — visible cost is part of
  the product. Zhang et al. 2022: givers rate high-priced gifts more favourably (4.84 vs 3.12).
- **Personalisation is the fix for the worst gifts.** Waldfogel 1993 ("Deadweight Loss of
  Christmas"): gifts from extended family destroy ~⅓ of their value. The grandparent buyer —
  exactly our Extended circle — has the most value to recover by giving the child's own art.
- **UK survey base rates (Deloitte 2015, YouGov n=1,560, verified from the original PDF):**
  1 in 5 interested consumers will pay a 20% premium; in clothing (most tolerant category)
  ~81% would pay SOME premium and only 12% nothing; #1 stated reason for wanting
  personalisation = "customised products can make great gifts" (50%); 48% would wait longer;
  **42% prefer to be led by the brand and choose from presets** — direct validation of our
  preset magic-edits design over blank-canvas configurators.
- **Budget headroom.** YouGov Nov 2025: 61% of UK parents of under-18s spend >£100 per child
  on Christmas gifts (27% >£200). SunLife: grandparents £97–126 per grandchild (£2.4–2.75bn
  UK total). Newborn photography (closest keepsake WTP proxy): £350–£1,000 routinely paid.

## 4. Counter-evidence & risks (the critic pass)
- **The value end fails, not the premium end:** Card Factory shut gettingpersonal.co.uk
  (Jan 2025). Wonderbly reached profit (>£60m sales, >£10m EBITDA, sold to Penguin Random House
  June 2025) via scale + 25% AOV growth + adult expansion — the category rewards AOV, not price cuts.
- **Repeat purchase is the engine:** Moonpig FY25 — ~89% of revenue from existing customers;
  occasion reminders drive ~40% of orders. A one-off keepsake must earn CAC back in 1–2 orders
  UNLESS the Keepsake Home creates repeat occasions (it is designed to — this is the moat).
- **Cost-of-living margin pressure:** ~⅔ of UK adults cutting Christmas spend; 51% of parents
  would consider a second-hand child's gift. Headline spend figures overstate resilience.
- **Checkout economics dominate price psychology:** Baymard (~50 studies): 70.2% average cart
  abandonment; #1 cause (39–48%) = extra costs at checkout. Our free-tracked-delivery-baked-in
  decision is directly validated.
- **Price endings:** Wadhwa & Zhang (JCR 2015): ROUND prices (£40) outperform charm endings for
  hedonic/emotional purchases; Choi et al. 2014 finds a countervailing 9-ending justification
  effect. → consider round pricing for the keepsake tier; A/B before committing.
- **Cold-start trust:** every premium price above is sustained by a large review base (23.9k,
  16.5k, 2.3k…). No evidence an unknown brand commands them day one → launch premium SKUs
  ~10–15% under the niche band and climb as review capital accrues.
- **Unmeasurable from secondary sources** (put in our own analytics): gift-buyer vs self-buyer
  mix; lifetime orders per family (drawing window ~ages 3–8); personalisation-flow abandonment
  (sunk-cost effect of uploading vs added friction); Q4 revenue concentration.

## 5. What this means for the range (recommended moves)
1. **We are priced at-or-below the drawing-keepsake niche across the board.** Headroom exists
   on Prodigi economics alone: kids tee → ~£24–26 (niche £26, organic-cotton story matches
   lukedrewthis exactly), adult tee → ~£32–35, mug → ~£18–20 with the story, storybook A4 →
   ~£29. This is margin available WITHOUT changing supplier.
2. **The Contrado signature tier (£28–36 garments) sits WITHIN the demonstrated band, not above
   it** — Picture This Clothing's $59 cut-and-sewn kids garment is the existence proof for
   exactly Contrado's all-over capability. The premium must be presented as the product
   ("cut and sewn in London from their drawing", edge-to-edge) — not as the same tee costing more.
3. **Premiumise the unanchored, keep the anchored honest:** card stays ~£4.95 (gateway), mug
   stays close to sane, push storybook/box/framed/blanket where no cheap reference exists.
4. **The high-margin white space above our range:** drawing→silver jewellery (£50 realised with
   23.9k reviews behind it) and drawing→plush (£169+ with 8-week waits) — future products that
   monetise willingness-to-pay we currently leave on the table. Both fit "keep the magic".
5. **Occasions engine = LTV:** birthday/Christmas reminders from the Keepsake Home mirror
   Moonpig's 40%-of-orders mechanism. Build the reminder loop before spending on acquisition.
6. **A/B on our own funnel beats any published elasticity number** — none exists for this niche.
   Instrument price tests per category once traffic allows.
