# C14 leaflets: the creative brief (second version, 17 Nov 2026)

The first version looked bad because it tried to say everything at once and never showed the one thing that sells
Snickerdoodle. This brief sets out what each leaflet must do, in order, and why. The designs follow it.

## What was wrong with version 1

1. **It never showed the magic.** Every photo was a finished product: a mug, a card on a mantelpiece, a bauble. Nothing
   showed that it came from a child's drawing. A parent glancing at it saw "personalised gifts", which Moonpig also sells.
2. **The weakest thing was the loudest.** The biggest sticker on the front said "your first card's on us". That's a
   £5.50 card, only on a signed-in first order with something else. It's a nice extra, but it isn't why anyone buys.
3. **Too many messages.** The front had a logo, a handwritten line, a headline, three photos, two stickers and a price
   strip, all about the same size, so nothing stood out. The back was a wall of text in seven sections.
4. **Wrong price beside the photo.** The flat snowman ornament was captioned "a bauble" next to "baubles from £19". Flat
   ornaments are the ceramic ornament at £21. The £19 bauble is the round one.
5. **It didn't look like Snickerdoodle's best work.** The approved social posts in `brand-assets/social` are far stronger:
   one big picture, one sentence-case headline, a handwritten paper tag, one tomato pill, kraft doodle paper and lots of air.
   Version 1 ignored them and invented a busier look.
6. **The question it answered was wrong.** A parent at a school gate has two seconds. The leaflet has to answer "what is
   this?" with a picture, not with words.

## The single idea

**Show the drawing next to the thing it became.** A wobbly crayon robin on a scrap of paper, a hand-drawn arrow, then the
same robin on a ceramic ornament hanging on a tree. Nobody needs to read anything to get it. Everything else on the leaflet
is there to make that picture easy to act on.

## The order a parent should take it in

| Second | Front of the leaflet | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | **The before → after picture** (drawing → ornament on the tree) | Says what we do with no words. It's the only thing they're sure to see. |
| 2–4 | **The headline: "Their drawing, on your tree."** (5 words, sentence case, as in the social posts) | Names the product and the feeling. |
| 4–6 | **Price and deadline**: "Christmas baubles from £19 · free UK delivery" and a tomato pill "order by sat 12 dec" | Price stops "that'll be expensive". The date makes it now or never. |
| then | **Logo and web address**, small | They need to know who it's from, but the brand isn't the hook yet. |

| Back of the leaflet | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Try it with their drawing, free."** plus one line on how: snap it on your phone, see it on everything in seconds, no account, no posting originals | The QR's promise has to be concrete and free. That's what gets a scan. "Scan to start creating" didn't say what happens. |
| **A big QR code** on a white card, labelled "scan to try it", with the web address under it | The one action. Bigger than anything else on the back. |
| **"Also made real"**: a row of four small product photos with prices (mug £18, cards £5.50, wrapping paper £23, 2027 calendar £22) | Shows the range and that it's affordable, without paragraphs. |
| **"Send granny a ready-to-buy link"** | Grandparents are half the Christmas buyers, and this is something no rival does. |
| **The free first card**, as a small coupon with the code | A sweetener and the tracking code, not the headline. |
| **Private by default, nothing ever public; last order dates**; small print | Reassurance for careful parents, and the conditions the rules require. |

## Words: what we say and don't

- **Say:** their drawing; made real; on your tree; try it free; snap it on your phone; no posting originals; order by sat
  12 dec; send granny a ready-to-buy link; private by default.
- **Don't say:** anything to a child; "AI"; "personalised gifts" (generic); "perfect gift" (empty); "limited time" (it isn't).
- **Case:** sentence case for headlines (as in the approved social posts and the site's own headings); lowercase for pills
  and tags.
- **One handwritten (Gaegu) moment per side:** the "made real!" note on the arrow (front), and the tag on the hero (back).

## The three leaflet families

- **A6 Christmas (FAIR, GATE):** hand-outs. Front: robin before → after, headline, price, pill. Back: try-it QR, product
  row, granny line, coupon, trust, dates.
- **A5 Christmas (DOOR, BAGS):** for when nobody is there to explain it. Front: the A6 front with more room, plus "for the
  grown-ups". Back: three steps, a big QR, product row, granny line, a "what happens to their drawings" box, coupon,
  dates.
- **A5 spring (BAGS):** book bags for Mother's Day.
  - **Front:** "Mother's Day, drawn by them." The family drawing taped down, with an arrow to a fan of the card, mug,
    cushion and framed print made from it. "For the grown-ups" sits at the top.
  - **Back:** what to do with the drawings in this very book bag: try it free, keep every one in a private keepsake box,
    and how to order.

## How we'll know it worked

Each leaflet has its own code and QR tag (see the report, section 5). Before printing, the design must pass:

- **A glance test:** cover the words and someone still knows what Snickerdoodle does.
- **An arm's-length test:** the headline and QR work at 60 cm.
- **A rules check:** nothing speaks to a child, every price is true on the live site, and every condition is printed.

## Changes after the independent critique (17 Nov, version 2.1)

A second designer reviewed version 2 cold. Every fix below went in:

| Critique | Fix |
|---|---|
| The front never says the preview is free | The front now carries the tomato promise "Scan to see their drawing on an ornament. Free, in seconds." beside its own QR. |
| The "after" is smaller than the "before" | The ornament photo is cropped 1.6× so the ornament fills about 70% of the frame. The drawing shrank. |
| "Ornament £21" next to "baubles from £19" and "on a bauble" | Named consistently: "Ornaments £21 · baubles £19"; the QR promise says "on an ornament". |
| The arrow lands on the photo's tape | Re-angled so it points at the ornament; cinnamon with a paper halo so it reads on kraft. |
| The spring headline asks mum to buy her own present | "For mum and granny, drawn by them." The drawing now has a white paper border so it reads as paper, not a print. The mismatched mug photo is gone from the front, and the promise says "on a card". |
| The coupon hides its condition | "First order? A card's on us with your first keepsake." plus "use code". |
| The A5 back is crowded | The subline is gone and the steps are one line each, so the QR card is bigger (51 mm). |
| Small print at 5.3–5.7 pt; tag sublines at 5.6 pt | Small print is 6.3–6.6 pt (and shorter); tags are 6.5–7 pt; captions 7–8 pt. |
| Things inside the 5 mm safe area; thin QR quiet zone | Everything moved inside, and `build.mjs` now warns if any text crosses the safe line. QRs have a 3 mm white border. |
| "No posting the original"; "treat them in a tap"; "See it on everything" | "You keep the original."; "Send granny a ready-to-buy link. She orders in a tap."; "See it on gifts". |
| Entry prices shown as fixed | "from" on mug, wrapping paper, calendar and jigsaw. |
| The "for the grown-ups" pill fights the logo | Now a light outline pill. |
