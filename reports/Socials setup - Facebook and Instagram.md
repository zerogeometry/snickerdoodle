## 1. The right structure

Meta's building blocks, from the bottom up:

| Thing | What it is | Who it belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| **Your personal Facebook profile** | Your normal Facebook. It's the key that opens everything else. Nobody sees it on the Page. | You, personally |
| **Facebook Page "Snickerdoodle"** | The shop's public face on Facebook | The business portfolio |
| **Meta business portfolio** (opened in Meta Business Suite) | The folder that owns the Page, the Instagram account and, later, any ad account | Launch Mode Ltd |
| **Instagram professional account (Business type)** | The shop's Instagram | The business portfolio, linked to the Page |

Why this order matters: if the Page and Instagram sit inside a business portfolio, losing access to one personal profile doesn't lose the business. A second admin can always get back in.

### Step 1 · Your turn: make a login email alias for social (5 min)

Make a new free alias, **social@snickerdoodle.co.uk**, on your Google Workspace user, the same way hello@ and support@ were made. Mail to it lands in your normal inbox.

- Use **social@** to log in to Instagram, as the business portfolio's business email, and for Meta's emails. Don't publish it anywhere.
- Use **hello@** as the public contact email on both profiles.
- Never use your personal email, or simon@launchmode, for the business accounts. A login address nobody knows is much harder to phish.

Go to [Google Admin → Users](https://admin.google.com/ac/users) → click your name → **User information** → **Alternate email addresses (email aliases)** → add `social`. Help: [Add an email alias (Google)](https://support.google.com/a/answer/33327).

### Step 2 · Your turn: get an authenticator app on your phone (3 min)

Install **Google Authenticator** or **Microsoft Authenticator** (both free). Codes from an app are safer than text messages, because a stolen SIM can't receive them.

### Step 3 · Your turn: lock down your own Facebook profile (10 min)

**Which profile:** your own, real, personal Facebook profile. Don't make a second "Snickerdoodle" person-profile. Meta's rules allow one real profile per person, and a fake one gets disabled, taking any Page it runs with it. Your profile's name never shows on the Page. Meta's [What to know before you create a Page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/366099230478737) confirms you need a profile to make one.

If you don't have a personal Facebook, make one with your real name and your personal email, and keep it plain.

On your profile, go to [Accounts Centre → Password and security](https://accountscenter.facebook.com/password_and_security):

1. **Two-factor authentication** → choose **Authentication app** → scan the code with the app from step 2. Help: [Two-factor authentication on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/help/148233965247823).
2. On the same screen, save your **recovery codes**. Print them, or put them in your password manager. Without them, a lost phone can mean a lost Page.
3. Optional: add a **passkey** (Face ID) on the same screen. That's quicker and phishing-proof.
4. Turn on **login alerts**.
5. Check your profile's mobile number is your current one.

**Keep it separate:** on your personal profile, set **Who can see your friends list** to *Only me*, and don't list Snickerdoodle as your workplace if you'd rather keep the two apart. You'll act **as the Page** whenever you post or reply for the shop (see step 4).

3. Give the backup admin **full control of the Page** too: **Accounts** → **Pages** → Snickerdoodle → **Assign people**.

**Traps, in one place:**

## 5. Domain verification and linking

### Step 18 · Your turn: verify snickerdoodle.co.uk with Meta (15 min, then wait)

**Why:** it proves to Meta that the website is yours. You'll need it before any shop, ad link or catalogue, and it stops anyone else claiming your links. Meta's help: [Verify your domain](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/321167023127050) · [Developer guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/sharing/domain-verification/verifying-your-domain).

**Use the DNS record, not the meta tag.** A DNS record is a 2-minute change in Netlify with no website deploy, and it survives any site rebuild. The meta-tag route would need a code change and a release.

## 6. Shops and tracking

### Facebook and Instagram Shops: not now; look again in January

**What a Shop is today in the UK:** a product catalogue on your Page and Instagram, where tapping a product sends the buyer to **your website** to pay. Paying inside Facebook or Instagram is US-only, and Meta has been closing it down even there since September 2025. Sources: [About Shops](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2343035149322466) · [Commerce eligibility](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1627591223954487) · [Checkout URL guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/commerce-platform/setup-checkout-url).

**Why wait:**

- **Eligibility:** Meta wants an "authentic, established presence, which may include a sufficient follower base", a verified domain and an approval review. A Page and Instagram opened this week will probably be turned down or held up until after Christmas.
- **Little gain:** UK buyers end up on your site either way. A link in the bio and link stickers on Stories do the same job for free, today.
- **Your products are personal:** every item needs the buyer's own drawing, so "tap to buy" from a catalogue photo still means "go to the site and upload". The catalogue photos would only ever be examples.

**Good news for later:** the Google feed you already have, `https://snickerdoodle.co.uk/feeds/google-merchant.xml`, is an RSS XML feed in Google's format. Meta's catalogues accept RSS XML and Google Merchant feeds, and can fetch them on a schedule ([Create a catalogue](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1275400645914358)). So a Shop needs **no new code**:

1. Domain verified (step 18).
2. [Commerce Manager](https://business.facebook.com/commerce/) → **Add catalogue** → **E-commerce** → **Data feed** → **Scheduled feed** → paste the feed address → daily.
3. **Create a shop** → checkout method **On your website**.

**Decision:** revisit in mid-January, once the accounts have a few hundred followers.

### Meta Pixel and Conversions API: not now, and not before Christmas

**Recommendation: don't add them.** Use the free alternative below.

**Why not:**

- **Consent first.** UK law (the ICO's rules on cookies, pixels and tags) says an advertising tag can't run until someone says yes, and saying no must be as easy. The **Conversions API** (sending sales to Meta from the server) needs that consent too; doing it server-side doesn't get round it. Your banner today offers *accept all* / *essential only*, and the cookies page promises that both set the same cookies, with no analytics and nothing used for advertising. A pixel would make that promise untrue.
- **No payoff at your budget.** The ads report (`reports/Christmas digital ads 2026.md`, PR #82) found that Meta ads cost 5–10 times more per family reached than parent newsletters. A pixel only pays off when you spend enough for Meta to learn from it: around 50 sales a week through ads.
- **Children's data.** Your site is full of pages a pixel must never touch (the studio, the keepsake home, gift links, Draw). Getting that boundary right needs care, not a pre-Christmas rush.

**What it would take, if you ever want it** (for the record, a 2027 job):

1. A new **marketing** choice in the cookie banner, separate from analytics, off by default, with an equal *no* button.
2. Update the cookies page, the Privacy Notice and the DPIA to name Meta as a recipient, and accept Meta's Business Tools Terms.
3. Load the pixel only after a yes, using Meta's consent switch (`fbq('consent','revoke')` until the yes, then `grant`) ([Meta's guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/meta-pixel/implementation/gdpr)). Withdrawing has to switch it off again.
4. Fire it **only** on shop pages and the order-complete page. Never on the studio, the keepsake home, gift links, family pages, /draw, the companion app or anything a child might use.
5. Send Meta only the order value and currency. **Never** a product image, a drawing, a child's name, personalised text or a basket of personal items. For the Conversions API: a hashed email only with marketing consent, never anything else about the order.
6. No "custom audiences" from your customer list, no lookalikes, no retargeting of families who uploaded drawings.

**What to do instead (free, today):** make a campaign code and use it in your social links, the same way the leaflet uses DRAWIT. The `partners` report then shows which sales came from social, with no tracking at all.

```
node --env-file=.env scripts/promo-codes.mjs create HELLO10 --percent 10 --partner "socials" --type CAMPAIGN
```

Then share `https://snickerdoodle.co.uk/start-creating?code=HELLO10` in posts and Stories ("10% off your first keepsake, just for saying hello"). Pick another code name if you like; 10% is margin-safe on every item per `briefs/DISCOUNTS.md`. **I'll do:** I can run that command for you once you say yes.

## 7. Safety and privacy rules for posting

Print this bit. It's the part that protects the brand.

### The five rules

1. **Never post a customer's child, their drawing or their name without the parent's clear written yes.** That means an email or a DM reply that says yes to the exact picture and the exact words. Keep it (star the email, or screenshot the DM into a "permissions" album). If they later say "please take it down", remove it the same day, everywhere, and reply to confirm.
2. **No surnames, ever.** First name and age only, and only if the parent wants them. The default is no name at all: "drawn by a little one, age 5".
3. **No faces, schools or places.** No children's faces, no school jumpers or logos, no street names, no house numbers on the drawing, no photos that show where a family lives. Hands and art only, the same rule as the creator brief.
4. **No drawings in DMs.** If someone sends you a photo of their child or their child's art, thank them and point them to the site's upload, where it stays private. Don't save it.
5. **Never message a child.** If an account looks like a child (talking about Draw, school, "my mum"), reply once with the saved reply below, then stop. Don't follow children back. Don't follow customers' personal accounts.

**Asking a parent (paste)**

```
we love this so much! would you be happy for us to share a photo of the {keepsake} on snickerdoodle's instagram and facebook? we'd only show the keepsake and the drawing: no faces, no surname, nothing about where you live. we can say "drawn by {first name}, age {age}", or no name at all if you'd prefer. totally fine to say no, and you can ask us to take it down any time. just reply "yes, happy for you to share" if that's ok. thank you! Simon x
```

**Saved reply for a child (paste)**

```
hi! 😊 this account is for grown-ups, so please ask a grown-up to get in touch with us at hello@snickerdoodle.co.uk. happy drawing! 🎨
```

### Hidden words (paste into both apps)

On Instagram: **Settings** → **Hidden Words** → **Manage custom words and phrases**. On Facebook: Page **Settings** → **Followers and public content** → **Hide comments containing certain words**. Facebook takes up to 1,000 words and hides spelling variations automatically ([help](https://www.facebook.com/help/131671940241729)); Instagram also hides them from message requests ([help](https://help.instagram.com/700284123459336)).

```
dm me, dm for, message me, inbox me, whatsapp, telegram, snapchat, snap me, kik, wickr, send pics, send a pic, more pics, private pics, how old, what school, which school, where do you live, your daughter, your son, cute girl, cute boy, sugar daddy, cash app, cashapp, paypal me, crypto, bitcoin, forex, investment, promote your page, collab dm, feature page, free followers, buy followers
```

Hidden comments aren't deleted: you can still read them (and unhide a genuine one) in each app's hidden comments view, so check it once a week. Both apps' built-in filters (*Hide comments*, *Advanced comment filtering*, the profanity filter) stay on too.

**I'll do:** if you want the site's own swear-word list (the D28 list in `src/server/textSafety.ts`) added as well, ask and I'll give it to you as one line ready to paste.

### Meta's rules that help you

- **Under-13s aren't allowed** on Facebook or Instagram (13 is the UK minimum). If you're fairly sure an account is a child under 13, report it: [Instagram](https://help.instagram.com/517920941588885) · [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/help/157793540954833). Don't engage or ask their age.
- **Teen accounts** (13–17) are private by default, adults can't message teens who don't follow them, and teens only get messages from people they follow or are connected to ([Meta, Jan 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/01/introducing-stricter-message-settings-for-teens-on-instagram-and-facebook/)). You never need to message one.
- **Ads can't be targeted at teens** by anything except age and location ([Meta's 2023 change](https://searchengineland.com/meta-introduces-new-ad-targeting-limits-for-teens-391259)). You won't target them at all.

### Ads rules for Snickerdoodle (for any boost, ever)

- **Age 25–65+** (never below 18). **Location: United Kingdom.** Set these yourself; Meta's automatic audience can't go below the age you set.
- **No "parents of toddlers / early school age" targeting.** It's a grey area under your own rule (no profiling of or about children), and the ads report left it as your decision, not in the plan. The default here is **off**.
- **No custom audiences from customer emails, no lookalikes, no retargeting** of site visitors.
- **Snickerdoodle Draw is a kids' app: advertise it only to parents.** Adults 25+, wording for grown-ups ("a calm, ad-free drawing app for your little one"), linking to snickerdoodle.co.uk/draw.
  - Never "ask your mum or dad to download": the UK ad code bans telling children to pester their parents ([CAP Code section 5](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/05.html)).
  - Don't use Meta's **app install** campaigns: they need Meta's tracking kit inside the app, and a Kids-category app shouldn't contain one ([Apple's guideline 1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#kids)). Draw rightly has none.
- Every ad follows [Meta's Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/).
