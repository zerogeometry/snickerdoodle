# Sourcing and shipping cost savings

*Turn on Pro now, move frames later.* Research report, 26 November 2026. Research only: no code, prices or suppliers were changed.

> **Exact figures are private.** This report goes in a public repo, so it never says what Snickerdoodle pays Prodigi for any product or post. It uses rounded bands only ("very large saving", "£300–£500 a month at 200 orders"). The per-item numbers behind every band are in the founder's private file `.prodigi-assets/SOURCING-SAVINGS-2026-11-26.md` (gitignored). Rival makers' prices below are their own public prices, read on 26 Nov 2026, with links. The full research notes, one file per maker group, are in [`sourcing-costs/research-notes/`](sourcing-costs/research-notes/).

The biggest and easiest saving is Prodigi Pro: one switch, no code. It is worth about £500 a month at 200 orders, if the live account is not on it already. The biggest saving that takes real work is moving framed prints to Gelato, which makes them in the UK for much less. That move brings a new company into contact with children's drawings, so it has to wait for the new year and your sign-off. Postage is the other lever. Today one phone case puts the whole basket on Express, and splitting each order by lab fixes that. No other maker beats Prodigi on most of the range once Pro is on, so the right plan is to keep Prodigi as the main maker and move one or two lines, not to switch wholesale.

## The short version

- **Check today whether the Prodigi account is on Pro.** If not, switch it on: about **£100 · £500 · £1,300 a month** at 50 · 200 · 500 orders, after the fee. That is 15% off products and 10% off clothing, with postage at full price. It costs £35 a month on the annual plan or £40 a month on the monthly plan, which you can cancel any time ([Prodigi Pro](https://www.prodigi.com/prodigi-pro/)).
- **Framed prints are where Prodigi is beaten.** Gelato makes framed prints in the UK, and the saving is very large on 11×14″, 12×16″ and 18×24″. That is worth about **£300–£500 a month at 200 orders**. The catches: no gold frame, thinner paper, no 16×24″ multi frame, and a new company that would see children's drawings. Order samples in January and build in February, but only if you approve Gelato as a new processor.
- **Email Prodigi now for a better frame price.** Use Gelato's public prices as the benchmark. If Prodigi closes half the gap, that is about **£150–£250 a month at 200 orders**, from one email. A bespoke deal can't be added on top of Pro, so only take one that beats Pro on the whole basket.
- **One phone case puts the whole basket on Express.** Express adds about £1–£8 to each of the other items. Sending each lab's items as their own Prodigi order, with only the case on Express, saves about **£35–£40 a month at 200 orders**. It also lets the case stay tracked when a calendar is in the basket. It's a code change, so it waits until after Christmas.
- **Most of the range is already as cheap at Prodigi as anywhere UK/EU,** once Pro is on. That covers mugs, tees, cushions, blankets, jigsaws, cards, playing cards, height charts, wrapping paper, placemats, the bauble and the watch strap.
- **Children's-data rule: every new maker is a stop-and-ask.** That means a new Article 28 processor (a signed data agreement under UK GDPR Article 28, called a "DPA" below), an update to the DPIA (our data-protection risk assessment), and UK/EU-only production promised in writing. Prodigi is the best data-protection fit of every maker checked. Gelato is the only rival that comes close.
- **Before Christmas:** Pro, the Prodigi email, and reading Prodigi's Christmas cut-off dates. **After Christmas:** Gelato samples and data questions, the per-lab order split, then frames to Gelato if approved.

### How a basket reaches Prodigi today

Each basket goes to Prodigi as **one order with one shipping method**. Prodigi then splits it into shipments, one per lab and parcel class. Everything goes Standard with one exception: **any basket with a phone case goes Express as a whole**, so the case is tracked, because its Standard route is often untracked Royal Mail 24 from uk8. In sandbox quotes on 5 Nov, that raised the other items' post by **about £1–£8 each**. The rise was biggest on socks, greetings cards and tees, and about £1 on mugs, ornaments, jigsaws and wrapping paper. A calendar can never go Express, so a case-plus-calendar basket goes Standard and the case travels untracked.

Items from the **same lab in the same parcel class share one shipment for about £1 more per extra item** (cards are a flat post). Items from different labs never share a parcel, and socks ship on their own even though they come from uk9 like the homeware. Prices to customers include free delivery, so every item carries its own single-item post. Customers get £3 off each extra item in the same parcel group, a "buy more & save" of 0–15% from the second of the same product, and 5% off three or more garments. Prodigi packs and posts, so there is no packaging cost of our own.

The private margins sheet says the products that **earn least** at the normal price are:

- the 30-piece jigsaw
- the plastic bauble
- the 24×32″ framed print
- the snap phone case
- the 110-piece jigsaw
- the 22″ one-sided cushion
- socks
- the adult tee in navy, grey or khaki (the cost loader stores the white price for those colours)

**Postage is the biggest share of cost** on the plastic bauble, playing cards, jigsaws, height charts, wrapping paper, socks, mugs, the heart cushion and the ceramic ornament.

## Gelato is the only rival that beats Prodigi on the big-ticket items

Five groups of makers were checked: Gelato; the Printful and Printify platforms; UK makers for hard goods, paper and textiles; EU phone-case makers; and printing networks such as CloudPrinter and Peecho. Each was judged on public price, UK postage, quality evidence, UK production, how well it connects to our site, and data protection. Only Gelato combines big savings, UK production and a usable data agreement. Most others fail on one of those three.

### Gelato: cheap, UK-made frames, but a network that can route abroad

Gelato's public UK catalogue prices its classic matte framed poster, ready to hang, at:

| 18×24″ | **£31.54** | £25.23 | £9.99 | £4.49 |

([Gelato framed poster](https://www.gelato.com/uk/custom/bestsellers/classic-matte-paper-wooden-framed-poster?region=UK), read 26 Nov 2026)

These frames are listed as produced in Great Britain among other countries. They are pine, in white, wood, dark wood or black, with **shatterproof plexiglass**, which suits a child's room. The paper is 170gsm matte. Gelato+ costs **$19.99 a month billed annually, or $29.99 monthly**. It gives "up to 33%" off, which is 20% on frames ([Gelato plans](https://www.gelato.com/uk/subscription-plans)). Gelato has **no gold frame and no true multi-photo frame**. A collage would be printed as one picture in an ordinary 30×30 cm (£17.57) or 60×90 cm (£56.65) frame, and there is no 16×24″ size.

Gelato also makes the two-tone mug, packs of 10 folded cards, A4 2027 calendars (no A5), iPhone-led phone cases, a 76 mm ceramic ornament and organic tees. It makes no cushions, jigsaws, blankets, socks, tea towels, playing cards, wrapping paper, placemats, watch straps, baubles or height charts.

On quality, Gelato's **Trustpilot score is 4.2 from 3,496 reviews**, 84% of them five-star ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/gelato.com), 26 Nov 2026). Complaints in the week of this research mention damaged posters, dull mug colours and Black Week stock-outs. Its quality guarantee covers framed posters, cards and calendars, but **not mugs, clothing or phone cases** ([Gelato guarantee](https://www.gelato.com/uk/legal/quality-and-delivery)).

Like Prodigi, Gelato **splits a basket into separate parcels when items are best made in different places**, and charges each parcel separately ([Gelato help](https://support.gelato.com/en/articles/8996103-what-happens-when-an-order-is-sent-to-multiple-print-locations)).

Gelato's order system is mature. Our site would send it orders with a picture link, get price quotes, and receive status messages back ([Gelato order docs](https://dashboard.gelato.com/docs/orders/v4/create/)). Two fit issues stand out. Gelato downloads each picture from a link we give it, so we would make a short-lived signed link per order. And its status messages have **no documented signature check** ([Gelato webhooks](https://dashboard.gelato.com/docs/webhooks/)), which our own rules require, so we'd need a workaround such as checking order status on a timer.

On data, Gelato has merchant Data Processing Terms (Nov 2024) and ISO 27001. Print files are stored on AWS in Ireland, and printers must delete files within 14 days of printing ([Gelato help](https://support.gelato.com/en/articles/8996124-where-are-the-print-files-stored-are-they-deleted-after-the-order-is-fulfilled)). But Gelato sends each order to "the closest production center", and says orders "may be shipped internationally even if a fulfillment center exists within the same country" ([Gelato tough case page](https://www.gelato.com/uk/custom/phone-cases/tough-case)). Its privacy policy also allows storage in the US ([Gelato privacy](https://www.gelato.com/uk/legal/privacy)). **For children's drawings, Gelato is usable only with UK-only routing promised in writing.**

## Cutting the post: one Express phone case taxes the whole basket

Prodigi chooses the carrier. Snickerdoodle chooses only the **shipping method per order** (Budget, Standard, Express and, for some UK labs, Overnight) and **which products go in which order** ([Prodigi shipping FAQ](https://www.prodigi.com/faq/shipping/)). So the levers are order structure, method choice and product choice. Carrier deals are not a lever.

### Split each basket into one Prodigi order per lab

The method is set once per order, and nothing stops us sending one customer's basket as several Prodigi orders. Items from different labs are already separate shipments with their own post, so sending **the phone case as its own Express order and everything else on Standard** should cost the same for the case. It also removes the **£1–£8 Express uplift** from every other item.

It fixes the calendar problem too: the case can go tracked even when a calendar is in the basket. And the same split lets **wrapping paper go on Budget, which is Evri 48: tracked, and cheaper than today's untracked Standard**. Playing cards can go on Budget too, which is Royal Mail 48 and cheaper, though slower.

The saving is about **£10 · £35–£40 · £90–£100 a month** at 50 · 200 · 500 orders. There is one edge case to check first: a watch strap and a case both come from uk6, and on Express they might share a parcel. Prodigi's quote service gives the price both ways before any order is placed ([Prodigi quotes API](https://apis.io/apis/prodigi/prodigi-quotes-api/)). There is also an unexplained "StandardPlus" method in Prodigi's order system that might give a tracked case without paying for Express. Ask Prodigi.

The cost is more Prodigi orders per basket. That means more tracking emails and statuses to keep in step in the admin, and it needs tests. **It's fulfilment code, so it waits until after Christmas.**

### Same-lab parcels and lab choice

Prodigi sends each item to the nearest lab that makes it. I found **no public setting to pin a product to a lab or to merge parcels across labs** ([Prodigi shipping FAQ](https://www.prodigi.com/faq/shipping/)). The cheap parcel is the same-lab parcel: one shipment for about £1 per extra item. That is why the uk9 Christmas bundle works. It is also why a cushion (uk3) plus a frame (gb3) costs two full posts.

Three things are worth asking Prodigi:

- Can **socks join the uk9 homeware parcel**? Today they ship on their own.
- Can the **plastic bauble or playing cards** (uk5) be made at uk9?
- Does a **"preferred facility"** setting exist for bespoke or Enterprise accounts?

Nothing public says yes to any of them. When we plan bundles and "buy more & save" offers, favouring uk9 products (mugs, coasters, tea towels, ornaments, placemats) keeps the extra items on cheap post.

### Royal Mail tracked or untracked

Six lines travel untracked on Standard today:

- greetings cards and card packs (Guernsey Post)
- the plastic bauble
- wrapping paper
- playing cards
- the phone case when it goes on Standard

Untracked Royal Mail is measurably less reliable this year. In 2025/26 first class arrived on time **75.7% of the time against a 93% target**, and Ofcom opened an investigation on 1 June 2026 ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/post/royal-mail/investigation-into-royal-mails-quality-of-service-performance-in-202526)). No one publishes a loss rate, only lateness. Tracked 24/48 includes compensation up to £150, against £20 for first or second class ([Royal Mail Business Price Guide, May 2026](https://www.royalmailtechnical.com/rmt_docs/Rate_Cards_May_2026/Business_Price_Guide_May_2026_v1.pdf)). At carrier level, tracking a letterbox-sized item costs only about **£1–£1.50 more**. Royal Mail's Tracked 48 Large Letter is about £2.85–£3.30 online, against £1.55–£1.75 untracked on contract (same guide; [Royal Mail Tracked 48](https://www.royalmail.com/sending/uk/tracked-48)).

Prodigi replaces orders not received within 30 days, except where the address was wrong ([Prodigi shipping FAQ](https://www.prodigi.com/faq/shipping/)). So a lost untracked parcel costs us a wait, support time, maybe a refund and, in December, a missed Christmas gift. It doesn't cost us a reprint. On that basis:

- Keep **cards** untracked: low value, flat post.
- Move **wrapping paper** to Budget, which is cheaper and tracked.
- Keep the **phone case tracked** through the split.
- Treat the untracked **bauble** as one more reason to think about dropping it.

### Prodigi's own options: Pro, branded packing and Enterprise

**Pro does not discount postage.** Neither the Pro page nor the FAQ mentions a shipping discount ([Prodigi Pro FAQ](https://www.prodigi.com/faq/prodigi-pro/)). So Pro cuts the product part of each order only.

Pro gives **75% off branded inserts**. Prodigi's public list prices are a sticker £1.00, or a postcard or flyer £2.00, against £0.25 / £0.50 on Pro. A black-and-white packing slip is free where supported. Cushions get the slip only, and snap cases get nothing ([Prodigi inserts](https://www.prodigi.com/branded-packaging-inserts/)). We add no inserts today, so this is a cost to avoid, not a saving. If a gift note is wanted, the free slip is the cheap route.

Above Pro there is a custom **Enterprise** plan with bespoke volume pricing. It has no published price or threshold ([Prodigi Pro page](https://www.prodigi.com/prodigi-pro/)), and a bespoke price **replaces** Pro's discount rather than adding to it ([Prodigi Pro FAQ](https://www.prodigi.com/faq/prodigi-pro/)).

### Christmas timing

Prodigi publishes "recommended (not guaranteed)" Christmas 2026 last order dates, but the table loads in a way that couldn't be read automatically ([Prodigi Christmas dates](https://www.prodigi.com/christmas-delivery-dates/)). Royal Mail's own recommended last posting dates are about:

- **17–18 Dec** for second class (sources differ by a day)
- **19 Dec** for Tracked 48
- **21 Dec** for first class

([Yorkshire Evening Post](https://www.yorkshireeveningpost.co.uk/business/consumer/royal-mail-christmas-posting-dates-2026-uk-international-deadlines-9346915); [Easyship](https://www.easyship.com/blog/holiday-shipping-deadline-uk))

Prodigi's dates will be earlier, because making the item comes first (1–4 days for most products). Budget post is slower, so moving playing cards to Budget is a January change, not a December one.

### These savings don't simply add up

Pro (#1) and a bespoke frame price (#3) are **either-or**: Prodigi won't stack them. So #3 only helps if Prodigi's bespoke offer beats 15% off across the whole basket, not just on frames. Moving frames to Gelato (#2) also takes frames out of Pro's 15%, so part of #2's band is already counted in #1. The two together save less than their bands added up.

Pro's break-even is low, though. On the annual plan it pays for itself at about **£233 a month of product spend** if VAT is reclaimed, or about £280 if it isn't. That is simple arithmetic on Prodigi's public £35 fee. So Pro stays worth having even with frames gone.
