# Data protection fit of print-on-demand makers for a UK shop printing children's drawings

Research date: 26 November 2026. All sources were accessed on 26 Nov 2026 unless stated. "Doc date" is the date printed on the document itself. Quotes are short phrases only. Research only: no sign-ups, forms or contact.

Method notes for the report writer:
- Gelato's legal and trust pages (gelato.com/legal/..., /trust) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch. Gelato's Data Processing Terms findings below come from search-result extracts of those pages, not a full read. Treat them as "stated, but should be re-read in full".
- Printful's legal documents are served as PDFs. I extracted their text locally (Data Processing Terms, Terms of Service, Privacy Policy), so those findings come from a full read.
- Teemill's and Printed.com's legal pages are rendered by script or iframe, and their body text could not be retrieved. Where I say "not found" for them, read it as "could not be read", not as "does not exist".

## 1. Is there a published DPA? Is the merchant the controller and the maker a processor?

### Takeaway
Only four makers publish a real merchant-facing DPA: Prodigi (its Peecho sister brand is not covered), Gelato, Printful and Printify. Prodigi's (May 2026) is the strongest and most specific for this use case. Inkthreadable, Contrado/Bags of Love, theprintspace/Creative Hub, CloudPrinter and Peecho publish only consumer-style privacy policies that cast themselves as controllers, and no DPA was found. Teemill and Printed.com could not be read.

### Cited Findings
**Prodigi (current maker)**
- Publishes a "Prodigi Group Data Processing Addendum", Version 1.0, effective 1 May 2026, at https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/. The merchant is the controller of "Merchant Customer Data" and Prodigi is the processor for providing the Services (cl. 2). Prodigi is a controller for its own account, billing, fraud, support and marketing data. — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- Processing is only on documented instructions (cl. 5.3). Purposes listed include routing, manufacture, dispatch, reprints, returns, QC and fraud (cl. 5.1, Sch. 1). — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- A companion "Subprocessor & Fulfilment Network Notice", v1.0, effective 1 May 2026, "forms part of" the DPA. — [Prodigi notice](https://www.prodigi.com/subprocessor-and-fulfilment-network-notice/)

**Gelato**
- Publishes "Data Processing Terms", with "Last Updated Date: 29 November 2024", in which Gelato acts as processor for the customer. A separate DPA exists for the GelatoConnect (print-factory software) product. — [Gelato Data Processing Terms](https://gelato.com/legal/data-processing-terms) (search extract; page 403); [GelatoConnect DPA](https://www.gelato.com/au/legal/data-processing-agreement-for-gelatoconnect)
- Per those terms, print service providers act as sub-processors, while logistics providers act as separate controllers. — [Gelato Data Processing Terms](https://gelato.com/legal/data-processing-terms) (search extract)

**Printful**
- Publishes "Data Processing Terms", Version 1.1, "Last updated on March 12, 2026" (v1.0 was 19 Sep 2025). They form part of the Terms of Service and "constitute a data processing agreement". Definitions of controller and processor follow the GDPR. — [Printful Data Processing Terms (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/data-processing-terms)
- Printful's Terms of Service were "Last updated on September 15, 2026". — [Printful ToS (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/terms-of-service)

**Printify**
- Publishes merchant "Data Processing Terms", v1.1, last updated 11 Mar 2026. Cl. 2.2 makes the merchant the Controller and Printify the Processor. — [Printify Data Processing Terms](https://printify.com/data-processing-terms/)
- Printify and Printful merged: the deal was announced 5 Nov 2024 and closed with integration from 20 Nov 2024. The brands stay separate under one holding company. — [Printful news](https://www.printful.com/news/merged+company+leadership+team); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/05/printful-and-printify-ink-a-merger-deal)

**Peecho**
- The privacy and cookie policy is dated "Last update: May 22, 2018". It does not define controller or processor roles for merchants' customers, and no DPA is mentioned. The site footer says Peecho is "A Prodigi Group print on demand company". — [Peecho privacy policy](https://www.peecho.com/privacy-policy)

**CloudPrinter**
- The privacy notice is effective "February, 16, 2022". It states no controller/processor role and mentions no DPA. — [Cloudprinter privacy policy](https://www.cloudprinter.com/privacy-policy)

**Inkthreadable**
- The privacy policy is effective 28 Feb 2024 and was last updated 3 Jul 2026. It says "we are a Data Controller of your data". No DPA is referenced, only a claim that the policy "is GDPR compliant". — [Inkthreadable privacy policy](https://www.inkthreadable.co.uk/privacy-policy)

**Teemill**
- The privacy policy text could not be retrieved (https://teemill.com/privacy-policy/ rendered only a page title; /privacy/ and /terms-of-service/ returned 404). Third-party stores' privacy notices describe Teemill Tech Ltd variously as "operator" of the store, a joint controller for limited activities, or a separate controller at checkout. This suggests Teemill is not set up as a pure processor. — [Help for Heroes trading privacy policy](https://www.helpforheroes.org.uk/policies/trading-privacy-policy/); [Nature Threads privacy policy](https://www.naturethreads.club/privacy-policy/)

**Contrado / Bags of Love**
- The privacy policy is "Version 2.0 | April 2026". It covers contrado.com, bagsoflove.co.uk and related sites, and names Contrado Imaging Ltd as data controller, registered with the ICO. It does not address any processor role or DPA for trade, dropship or API customers. — [Contrado privacy & cookies](https://www.contrado.com/privacy-cookies)

**theprintspace / Creative Hub**
- The privacy policy says Printspace is "the controller and responsible for your personal data". The page states "last updated in September 2019". No DPA is mentioned. — [theprintspace privacy policy](https://www.theprintspace.co.uk/privacy-policy/); [creativehub privacy policy](https://creativehub.io/privacy-policy/)
- A help article updated 19 Nov 2026 says customer data "is owned by you, the store owner". It also says theprintspace uses it only "to process and ship the order and do not pass it on". It does not use processor or controller terms. — [theprintspace help: who owns customer data](https://www.theprintspace.co.uk/help/knowledge-base/who-owns-the-customer-data-with-shopify-sales/)

**Printed.com**
- The privacy and terms pages are script/iframe rendered, and no text was retrievable (https://www.printed.com/privacy-policy, /terms-and-conditions). The page lists theteam@printed.com as a contact. — [Printed.com terms page](https://www.printed.com/terms-and-conditions)

### Inferences
- Only Prodigi, Gelato, Printful and Printify meet the shop's "Article 28 DPA in place" rule out of the box, through online terms accepted on sign-up. For the others, the shop would have to ask for and sign a bespoke DPA. Their own policies cast them as controllers, which is the wrong posture for children's images the shop controls.
- Peecho is in the Prodigi group, but its 2018 policy does not reference the Prodigi Group DPA. Whether the May 2026 Group DPA covers Peecho orders is unconfirmed.
- Overall fit snapshot, measured against the shop's rules:
  - **Prodigi:** strong. It has a DPA, an explicit no-AI-training clause, a 45-day file deletion rule, on-request deletion and SCC/IDTA transfers. Its weak points are unnamed partners, US/Australia locations, no published certification and no routing control.
  - **Gelato:** good on paper. It has a DPA, ISO 27001, AWS Ireland storage and a 14-day printer deletion rule. Its weak points are its routing network, possible US printers and confidential partner identities.
  - **Printful:** mixed. It has a DPA, but also a broad perpetual licence to use Content to "promote" and "improve" the Services, US parent access, and a sub-processor list only behind login.
  - **Printify:** weakest of the four with a DPA. It has a broad perpetual marketing/"developing" licence, a global third-party provider network, and a list available only on request.
  - **Others:** controller-only privacy policies and no DPA found. Each needs a negotiated DPA before use.

### Gaps
- No sub-processor list or DPA text was found for Teemill or Printed.com, because their pages could not be read.
- It is unconfirmed whether Contrado, Inkthreadable or theprintspace will sign a merchant DPA on request. Answering that would mean contacting them, which was out of scope.
- The full text of Gelato's Data Processing Terms (Appendix 2 sub-processor list) was not readable because of the 403.

## 2. Where are print files and order data stored and processed? Sub-processor list? Transfers outside UK/EU and on what basis?

### Takeaway
Gelato is the only maker that states a specific in-region store for print files (AWS Ireland). Prodigi lists UK, EEA, US, Australia and "other" locations and does not name partners. Printful and Printify route data through a US parent and Latvian, Polish and Spanish affiliates. None of the smaller UK makers names a hosting region. Transfer mechanisms are stated only by Prodigi (SCCs, UK Addendum and IDTA), Printful/Printify (SCCs including UK clauses) and Contrado (IDTA or SCCs with Addendum, plus DPF for Google). theprintspace still cites the defunct Privacy Shield.

### Cited Findings
**Prodigi**
- Production and fulfilment partners receive name, address, order details, product configuration, artwork and shipping data. Their typical locations are "UK, EEA, US, Australia and other fulfilment regions". Cloud hosting providers hold platform, order, artwork and log data in "UK, EEA, US and other service regions". No providers are named. — [Prodigi notice](https://www.prodigi.com/subprocessor-and-fulfilment-network-notice/)
- Production partners are not publicly named, citing DPA cl. 8.11 and the commercial sensitivity of partner identity and routing. More information may be given where law requires, or under an enterprise agreement. — [Prodigi notice](https://www.prodigi.com/subprocessor-and-fulfilment-network-notice/); [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- For transfers, Prodigi says it may use an adequacy decision, EU SCCs (Modules 2 and 3), the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, the UK IDTA, or another lawful mechanism (cl. 14). The merchant authorises Prodigi to enter transfer mechanisms with subprocessors (cl. 14.5). — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- Merchants can object to a material new subprocessor within 30 days, on reasonable data-protection grounds (cl. 8.8). The remedy is at Prodigi's discretion and may be letting the merchant stop the affected service (cl. 8.9). Merchants can subscribe to change notices via dpo@prodigi.com. — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)

**Gelato**
- Print files are stored with "AWS storage in Ireland" (help article dated 28 Oct 2025). — [Gelato help centre](https://support.gelato.com/en/articles/8996124-where-are-the-print-files-stored-are-they-deleted-after-the-order-is-fulfilled)
- The consumer privacy policy says personal data is stored with cloud providers "in the United States and Europe". Orders go to the printer closest to the delivery address, which may mean a transfer out of the EU. For US printers, Gelato says it can verify certification under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. For other non-EU printers, it relies on EU SCCs. — [Gelato privacy policy](https://www.gelato.com/uk/legal/privacy) (search extract)
- The Data Processing Terms say an up-to-date sub-processor list is kept on the platform as Appendix 2, and that identities "may be kept confidential". Customers can object, but Gelato "may then not be able to provide the Services". — [Gelato Data Processing Terms](https://gelato.com/legal/data-processing-terms) (search extract)

**Printful**
- Data "may be transferred or accessed by our parent company in the United States" and by affiliates in "Latvia, Poland, Spain, and the UK" (privacy policy last updated 12 Mar 2026). — [Printful privacy policy (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/privacy)
- The Data Processing Terms rely on EU SCCs, or "equivalent clauses issued by the relevant competent authority of the UK". Processing location is "as notified by us". The supervisory authority is Latvia's Data State Inspectorate. For the full sub-processor list, merchants must "login (registered users)" or complete a form. — [Printful Data Processing Terms (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/data-processing-terms)

**Printify**
- Sub-processors "vary case by case" and include cloud hosting, infrastructure and "fulfillment partners". The list (identities, services, locations) is available by email request (cl. 3.6, 7.1–7.2). — [Printify Data Processing Terms](https://printify.com/data-processing-terms/)
- Transfers use EU SCCs (Module 2; Module 3 for onward transfers) and UK standard contractual clauses (cl. 1.4, 7.4–7.5). No UK Addendum, IDTA or DPF is mentioned. — [Printify Data Processing Terms](https://printify.com/data-processing-terms/)
- The supplier DPA (v1.00, 19 Sep 2025) applies EU SCCs Modules 2/3 and UK SCCs (ICO Version A1.0), and requires a transfer impact assessment. Personal data may not leave China without Printify's written authorisation, which implies some providers are in China. — [Printify Supplier DPA](https://printify.com/supplier-data-protection-addendum/)

**Peecho**
- "We store all data in private Amazon buckets". No region is stated. Some service providers are in the US and New Zealand, under EU SCCs. — [Peecho privacy policy](https://www.peecho.com/privacy-policy)

**CloudPrinter**
- Data may be "transferred or accessed by Cloudprinter.com, its affiliated companies, and subsidiaries around the world". It promises "necessary measures" but names no mechanism. Print partners are not listed among recipients. — [Cloudprinter privacy policy](https://www.cloudprinter.com/privacy-policy)

**Inkthreadable**
- Data is transferred to and processed in the UK. Transfers elsewhere happen only if "adequate controls" exist, with no countries or mechanisms named. — [Inkthreadable privacy policy](https://www.inkthreadable.co.uk/privacy-policy)

**Contrado / Bags of Love**
- Named transfers are Google (US, SCCs plus DPF), Meta (US, SCCs) and an engineering team in Ahmedabad, India, which has access under contract and access controls. Safeguards include UK IDTA or EU SCCs with the UK Addendum. Hosting region is not stated. — [Contrado privacy & cookies](https://www.contrado.com/privacy-cookies)

**theprintspace**
- "Many of our external third parties are based outside the EEA". The stated safeguards are EC-approved contracts and "Privacy Shield" for US providers. Privacy Shield was invalidated in 2020, so the policy (dated Sep 2019) is out of date. — [theprintspace privacy policy](https://www.theprintspace.co.uk/privacy-policy/)

### Inferences
- For a "UK/EU-first" children's-image rule, Gelato's AWS Ireland storage is the clearest statement of in-region storage. But its print routing can still send files to US or other printers.
- Prodigi's own notice openly lists the US and Australia as fulfilment regions and the US as a hosting region. UK/EU-only processing therefore cannot be assumed without a contractual restriction.
- Printful/Printify data is accessible to a US parent. Printify's supplier DPA references China, so its provider pool is global.
- Contrado's India-based engineering access is relevant if engineers can reach the artwork store. The policy does not say they can't.

### Gaps
- No maker publicly names its cloud region for artwork except Gelato (Ireland) and Peecho (Amazon, region unstated).
- I could not view Gelato's Appendix 2, Printful's logged-in sub-processor list, or Printify's emailed list.
- Whether Prodigi's own UK site, Peecho or Gelato's UK printers handle UK orders in practice is not stated publicly.

## 3. How long are print files and customer data kept? Can the merchant request deletion (API or support)? Any automatic deletion?

### Takeaway
Prodigi is the only maker with a hard, published file-deletion clock: 45 days after dispatch, or 45 days after upload if unused. Merchants can request deletion by email with written confirmation in 7 business days, and backups roll over within 60 days. Gelato's printers must delete files 14 days after printing, but the merchant controls how long files sit in the Gelato account. Printify's supplier DPA anonymises order data 30 days after delivery. The rest give only "as long as necessary" language, and Contrado keeps uploaded artwork for the life of the account.

### Cited Findings
**Prodigi**
- Image and artwork files are deleted 45 days after dispatch, or 45 days after upload if unused, to allow reprints (cl. 12.1). Merchants may request deletion at any time via dpo@prodigi.com, per order, per file or for all files, and Prodigi confirms in writing within 7 business days (cl. 12.2). — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- Fulfilment partners delete files after production and dispatch (cl. 12.3). Backups are overwritten within 60 days (cl. 12.4). Order, invoice and support records have no fixed period (cl. 12.5, 12.7). — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- The DPA does not mention an API deletion endpoint, only email and dashboard options (cl. 12.6). — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)

**Gelato**
- "Our printers also have a contractual obligation to delete your print files within 14 days of printing". It also says "You control how long files are stored in your account" and that an automated deletion process exists (28 Oct 2025). — [Gelato help centre](https://support.gelato.com/en/articles/8996124-where-are-the-print-files-stored-are-they-deleted-after-the-order-is-fulfilled)
- Data may remain in backups after deletion, deleted per ordinary backup routines "without using the personal data for any purpose". — [Gelato Data Processing Terms](https://gelato.com/legal/data-processing-terms) (search extract)

**Printful**
- Retention is "for the duration of the Agreement until deletion following instruction of the Merchant". Order data is deleted "according to requirements of the platform from which Personal Data was collected". On termination, Printful will "return or delete any Personal Data without keeping a copy" and confirm in writing on request. — [Printful Data Processing Terms (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/data-processing-terms)
- The privacy policy says copies of merchants' customers' data may be kept after account deletion for legal, fraud and legitimate-interest purposes. — [Printful privacy policy (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/privacy)

**Printify**
- Merchant terms: on termination, data is returned or deleted "without keeping a copy", and order data follows the source platform's deletion rules. — [Printify Data Processing Terms](https://printify.com/data-processing-terms/)
- Supplier DPA: order fulfilment data must be anonymised "within thirty (30) days after delivery or dispatch". Backups over 60 days old are deleted, and data is deleted or returned within 15 days of termination. Design or print files are not specifically addressed. — [Printify Supplier DPA](https://printify.com/supplier-data-protection-addendum/)
- The ToS says account deletion is irreversible and sets no design-file retention period (last updated 17 Sep 2026). — [Printify Terms of Service](https://printify.com/terms-of-service/)

**Peecho**
- Data is kept "as long as we reasonably consider it to be necessary", and account data until five years of inactivity. No print-file period is stated. — [Peecho privacy policy](https://www.peecho.com/privacy-policy)

**CloudPrinter**
- Data is kept "for as long as your relationship with us is active", then as reasonably necessary. Print files are not mentioned. — [Cloudprinter privacy policy](https://www.cloudprinter.com/privacy-policy)

**Inkthreadable**
- Data is kept "only for as long as is necessary", with VAT records kept 7 years. "Please note that not all data can be deleted". Unshipped orders can't be deleted until marked shipped. — [Inkthreadable privacy policy](https://www.inkthreadable.co.uk/privacy-policy)

**Contrado / Bags of Love**
- Uploaded designs and artwork are kept for the "Duration of account (you can delete anytime)". Orders are kept 7 years. Account data is kept for account life plus 3 years, and inactive accounts are deleted after 5 years. Requests go to dataprotection@contrado.com, answered within one month. — [Contrado privacy & cookies](https://www.contrado.com/privacy-cookies)

**theprintspace**
- Data is kept "as long as reasonably necessary", and a retention policy is available on request. — [theprintspace privacy policy](https://www.theprintspace.co.uk/privacy-policy/)

### Inferences
- Prodigi's 45-day rule, plus on-demand deletion, plus partner deletion after dispatch, fits the shop's "no retention beyond fulfilment" rule best. The shop's own "fulfilment + 30d" source-upload purge would sit alongside it. The shop could also email a deletion request per order once a reprint window closes.
- Gelato's 14-day printer rule is shorter than Prodigi's. But files in the Gelato account persist until the merchant deletes them, so the shop would need to delete via the API or dashboard. API support for this is not confirmed in the sources.
- No maker documents an API deletion endpoint in the sources read.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed API-based file deletion for any maker.
- Printful's and Printify's actual print-file retention periods inside their own systems are unstated.
- Retention for Teemill and Printed.com is unknown, because their pages could not be read.

## 4. Do the terms let the maker use uploaded images for marketing, product improvement or AI/ML training? Any opt-out?

### Takeaway
Prodigi is the only maker that expressly bans AI/ML training on merchant data, artwork included. Printful and Printify both take a broad, perpetual, sublicensable licence over uploaded Content that includes promoting and improving the service. That is a direct conflict with the shop's "no marketing or training use" rule for children's drawings, and no opt-out was found. The UK makers' policies are mostly silent on AI.

### Cited Findings
**Prodigi**
- Prodigi will not use Merchant Customer Data, including artwork, to "train, fine-tune or otherwise improve any artificial intelligence or machine learning model" (cl. 5.5). Subprocessors are contractually barred from the same. — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- Prodigi "does not sell Merchant Customer Data" and does not market directly to merchants' customers. — [Prodigi notice](https://www.prodigi.com/subprocessor-and-fulfilment-network-notice/)
- Non-AI "product improvement" is not expressly prohibited, only limited by the documented-instructions rule. Prodigi's Terms of Use (for any artwork licence) were not reviewed. — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)

**Printful**
- ToS (15 Sep 2026): the user grants a "non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free, irrevocable, sub-licensable, perpetual license" to use, edit and reproduce Content to provide the Services, "improve the quality of the Services and to promote Printful", across "advertising medium and/or social media". The section summary says "We'll only use the content you submit to provide you with our services". This contradicts the licence text. — [Printful ToS (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/terms-of-service)
- The Data Processing Terms bar selling or sharing and use "outside of the direct business relationship". But the merchant "instructs us to aggregate, de-identify, or anonymize" personal data for other purposes. — [Printful Data Processing Terms (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/data-processing-terms)
- No express AI/ML training clause, permission or prohibition was found in the ToS or Data Processing Terms. — [Printful ToS (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/terms-of-service)

**Printify**
- ToS §H.8 (17 Sep 2026): the licence is "non-exclusive, transferable, sublicensable, royalty-free, perpetual and worldwide". It covers "operating, providing, developing, improving the quality of and promoting the Service", in any channel including advertising and social media. AI training is not mentioned. — [Printify Terms of Service](https://printify.com/terms-of-service/)
- The Data Processing Terms permit aggregation, de-identification and anonymisation for other purposes (cl. 9). — [Printify Data Processing Terms](https://printify.com/data-processing-terms/)

**Gelato**
- No statement on AI training or marketing use of uploaded images was found in the extracts retrieved. Full terms were blocked (403). — [Gelato Data Processing Terms](https://gelato.com/legal/data-processing-terms)

**Peecho**
- "We will never sell your Personal Information to a third party". Marketing needs consent. AI is not mentioned. — [Peecho privacy policy](https://www.peecho.com/privacy-policy)

**CloudPrinter**
- Merchant emails may be used for newsletters and ads. Website data may be combined with third-party data for personalised advertising. Uploaded files and AI are not mentioned. — [Cloudprinter privacy policy](https://www.cloudprinter.com/privacy-policy)

**Inkthreadable**
- The policy is silent on use of uploaded designs and on AI. — [Inkthreadable privacy policy](https://www.inkthreadable.co.uk/privacy-policy)

**Contrado / Bags of Love**
- Uploaded artwork is processed "only to fulfill your order". The policy's AI/ML section covers browsing and purchase data, such as recommendations and fraud scoring, not images. — [Contrado privacy & cookies](https://www.contrado.com/privacy-cookies)

**theprintspace**
- Image Data is used to process orders and provide services, and is shown to other users only on opt-in. Promotions do not use Image Data. AI is not mentioned. — [theprintspace privacy policy](https://www.theprintspace.co.uk/privacy-policy/)

### Inferences
- Printful and Printify's licences, which are perpetual and allow use to promote the service, are incompatible with children's drawings unless overridden. For example, a negotiated DPA term could prevail, since both sets of Data Processing Terms say they govern where they conflict with the main agreement for personal data. A child's drawing linked to a family is personal data, but the licence clause sits in the ToS, so the outcome is legally uncertain. Seek advice before relying on this.
- Prodigi's cl. 5.5 is a strong, specific clause. Including "subprocessors" in it covers the unnamed print partners.

### Gaps
- Prodigi's Terms of Use licence wording was not read.
- Gelato's position on marketing or AI use of uploads was not verified, because the pages were blocked.
- No maker offers a documented opt-out toggle.

## 5. Security certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2, Cyber Essentials)

### Takeaway
Gelato is the only maker with a published ISO/IEC 27001 certification (announced Oct 2023, audited by EY CertifyPoint). Prodigi's DPA lists security measures but no certification. None was found for Printful, Printify, Peecho, CloudPrinter, Inkthreadable, Contrado or theprintspace beyond PCI DSS via payment partners.

### Cited Findings
- **Gelato:** announced ISO/IEC 27001 certification in Oct 2023, with the audit by EY CertifyPoint. It says the certification covers "all core business processes". GelatoConnect pages claim TLS 1.2+ and annual penetration testing. — [Gelato ISO news](https://gelato.com/news/gelato-achieves-iso-iec-27001-certification); [GelatoConnect security](https://www.gelato.com/connect/security); [Gelato UK trust](https://www.gelato.com/uk/trust) (search extracts)
- **Prodigi:** Sch. 2 lists least-privilege access, MFA for admin and remote production access, encryption in transit, logging, vulnerability management and incident response. No ISO 27001, SOC 2 or Cyber Essentials is mentioned, and encryption at rest is not stated. On-site audits "are not available for standard self-serve accounts". — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- A search for Prodigi plus Cyber Essentials or ISO 27001 returned no Prodigi-specific result. — (search, 26 Nov 2026; no source)
- **Printful:** the Data Processing Terms annex requires AES encryption at rest and TLS 1.2+ in transit. Merchants can audit no more than once every 2 years. No certification is named. — [Printful Data Processing Terms (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/data-processing-terms)
- **Printify:** Annex 2 requires encryption in transit and at rest, at least annual vulnerability testing, and MFA. The supplier DPA accepts "a valid ISO 27001 certificate" as proof for print providers. Printify's own certification is not stated. — [Printify Data Processing Terms](https://printify.com/data-processing-terms/); [Printify Supplier DPA](https://printify.com/supplier-data-protection-addendum/)
- **Peecho** (encryption in transit and at rest; no certification) and **CloudPrinter** (SSL only): no certification is mentioned. — [Peecho](https://www.peecho.com/privacy-policy); [Cloudprinter](https://www.cloudprinter.com/privacy-policy)
- **Inkthreadable** and **Contrado** mention only PCI-DSS via payment processors. Contrado states encryption in transit and at rest. — [Inkthreadable](https://www.inkthreadable.co.uk/privacy-policy); [Contrado](https://www.contrado.com/privacy-cookies)

### Inferences
- If a certification is required for the children's-data DPIA, Gelato is the only maker that clears it publicly today. For Prodigi, the shop would need to rely on the DPA's security schedule, or ask for a security summary (cl. 13 offers summaries and questionnaires).

### Gaps
- No certificate registries were checked: the IASME Cyber Essentials search and the ISO certificate database were not queried. Certifications may exist but go unpublicised, especially for UK makers.
- Teemill and Printed.com could not be checked.

## 6. Routing networks (Printify, CloudPrinter, Gelato, Peecho, and Prodigi in practice): does the image go to an independent third-party printer, are partners bound by equivalent terms, and can routing be restricted to UK/EU?

### Takeaway
All five route artwork to third-party production partners. Printify binds its providers with the most detailed published supplier DPA: 72-hour breach notice, 30-day anonymisation, SCCs, and the merchant picks the provider. Gelato treats printers as sub-processors with written flow-down and a 14-day deletion rule, but routes to the "closest" printer. Prodigi requires only "appropriate" protection from unnamed partners. CloudPrinter and Peecho publish nothing on partner terms. No maker publicly offers a merchant switch to restrict routing to UK/EU, except Printify, where the merchant chooses the provider per product.

### Cited Findings
**Prodigi**
- Prodigi uses third-party manufacturing and dispatch partners in the UK, EEA, US, Australia and elsewhere, and does not name them. — [Prodigi notice](https://www.prodigi.com/subprocessor-and-fulfilment-network-notice/)
- Subprocessors must carry obligations "designed to provide an appropriate level of protection" (cl. 8.5). This is not an "equivalent terms" requirement. Prodigi remains liable (cl. 8.6). — [Prodigi DPA](https://www.prodigi.com/dpa/)
- No merchant routing restriction is described. The only control is the 30-day objection to new subprocessors. — [Prodigi notice](https://www.prodigi.com/subprocessor-and-fulfilment-network-notice/)

**Gelato**
- Print service providers are sub-processors. A written agreement imposing data-protection obligations is required, Gelato stays "fully responsible", and the agreement is available on request with commercial terms redacted. — [Gelato Data Processing Terms](https://gelato.com/legal/data-processing-terms) (search extract)
- Orders go to the printer closest to the delivery address, which may be outside the EU, with DPF or SCCs for those printers. — [Gelato privacy policy](https://www.gelato.com/uk/legal/privacy) (search extract)
- Printers must delete files within 14 days of printing. — [Gelato help centre](https://support.gelato.com/en/articles/8996124-where-are-the-print-files-stored-are-they-deleted-after-the-order-is-fulfilled)

**Printify**
- Providers include "third-party contract manufacturers". "Providers alone are responsible for their Products". Users should do "their own due diligence in selecting the most suitable Provider". — [Printify Terms of Service](https://printify.com/terms-of-service/)
- Merchant Data Processing Terms cl. 7.3: sub-processors are bound by obligations "no less onerous" than Printify's. — [Printify Data Processing Terms](https://printify.com/data-processing-terms/)
- Supplier DPA (19 Sep 2025): suppliers are processors, or sub-processors where Printify is processor. Processing is only as necessary for the Agreement, breach notice is required "not later than 72 hours", order data is anonymised within 30 days, and Printify can audit annually. — [Printify Supplier DPA](https://printify.com/supplier-data-protection-addendum/)

**CloudPrinter**
- The privacy notice does not list print partners among recipients or describe their terms. Cloudprinter "remains responsible" for its third-party service providers. — [Cloudprinter privacy policy](https://www.cloudprinter.com/privacy-policy)

**Peecho**
- Content may be seen by "the printer that prints it". Printers are not named and their terms are not described. — [Peecho privacy policy](https://www.peecho.com/privacy-policy)

**Printful** (mostly own facilities, not a pure network)
- Sub-processors must have written contracts with "at least the same level" of protection (Annex 10.3). — [Printful Data Processing Terms (PDF)](https://www.printful.com/policies/download/data-processing-terms)

### Inferences
- Printify's provider-choice model is the only way, among the networks, to restrict production to a named UK/EU provider at product level. But Printify's broad ToS content licence and US parent remain issues.
- With Gelato and Prodigi, a UK customer's order will most likely be made in the UK, but neither offers a documented guarantee. A written routing restriction would need to be negotiated, for example through Prodigi's enterprise route or a Gelato custom agreement.
- With CloudPrinter and Peecho, it cannot be verified from public documents who prints the child's image or under what terms.

### Gaps
- No public documentation was found of any "restrict to region" setting in the Gelato, Prodigi, CloudPrinter or Peecho APIs or dashboards.
- Whether Gelato's or Prodigi's UK-delivered orders ever route outside the UK/EU is not documented.

## 7. Known breaches or ICO actions

### Takeaway
No reported data breach or ICO enforcement action was found for any of the 11 makers. The only print-on-demand breach that surfaced was Spreadshirt/Spreadshop, which is not on the list. Gelato self-reports "zero major data security incidents" in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- A search for breaches at Printful, Printify or Gelato returned only policy documents and reviews, not incidents. A Shopify community post complains of a Printful integration stripping address data, which is an operational issue, not a security breach. — [Shopify community post](https://community.shopify.com/t/warning-printful-integration-stripped-critical-address-data-zero-support-response/586701)
- Spreadshirt/Spreadshop (not on the list) suffered a data breach affecting customers, partners and employees. The article is undated in the results. — [Graham Cluley](https://grahamcluley.com/spreadshop-hacked-t-shirt-lovers-warned-of-considerably-vicious-data-breach/)
- Gelato's 2025 sustainability report claims it is ISO 27001 certified with zero major data security incidents in 2025. This is self-reported. — [Gelato sustainability report 2025](https://www.gelato.com/connect/sustainability-report-2025) (search extract)
- An ICO enforcement search returned no print-on-demand companies. — [ICO enforcement](https://ico.org.uk/action-weve-taken/enforcement/)

### Inferences
- The absence of findings is weak evidence, since smaller makers' incidents may not be reported publicly.

### Gaps
- The ICO enforcement register was not searched by each company name. Only a general search was run.
- No news searches were run for Prodigi, Teemill, Contrado, Inkthreadable, theprintspace, Printed.com, CloudPrinter or Peecho individually, because of the tool budget.
