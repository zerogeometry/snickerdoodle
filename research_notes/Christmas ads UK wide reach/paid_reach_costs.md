# Paid reach costs in the UK, 2–14 Dec 2026: which channel reaches the most parents of 0–10s and grandparents per £

Researched 18 Nov 2026. About 25 searches/fetches. Labels: **(S)** = published figure with a URL; **(E)** = my estimate or arithmetic, not published. Where a source gives US dollars it says so. Exchange rate: $1 = £0.77 (the same assumption as `research_notes/Christmas digital ads 2026/paid_social_baseline.md`, which this file builds on and does not repeat in full).

Unit economics from the brief: an order leaves about £10–£14 profit (frame-led about £14). No ad pixel, so no platform can optimise for purchases. Measurement is by promo code, UTM links and click reports only. Ad window **2–12 Dec for most gifts (11 days), to 14 Dec for frames/blankets/cushions (13 days)**. Black Friday 2026 is 27 Nov and Cyber Monday 30 Nov, so the window starts just after the most expensive week (see the baseline file: 2025 Meta CPMs were still about +20% above baseline on 3 Dec).

Source quality: almost no platform publishes UK CPMs. Nearly every number below is from a third-party benchmark tool or an agency blog. Meta's help pages return no text to the fetch tool, so Meta rules are second-hand. Treat all CPMs as ranges, not quotes.

## 1. Meta (Facebook + Instagram): UK CPM and CPC in December, CPM by objective, what £150 / £300 buys, setup limits

### Takeaway
UK Meta CPMs roughly double from October to December: about **£9.80 in Oct 2025 vs about £19.60 in Dec 2025 (median, all objectives)**. Reach/awareness objectives are the cheapest way to buy unique people: published UK awareness CPMs are about **£4–£8** (year-round), so a December Reach campaign is plausibly **£6–£12 per 1,000 impressions (E)**. At that rate, **£150 reaches about 12,000–25,000 UK adults and £300 about 25,000–50,000 (E)**. A Traffic (link-click) campaign reaches about a third as many people but delivers clicks you can count.

### Cited Findings
- **UK monthly CPM, all objectives (Superads tool, GB accounts, Jul 2025–Jul 2026):** Oct 2025 = 13.20 (the year's low); **Dec 2025 = 26.48 (the year's high, about 31% above global)**; Mar 2026 = 14.42; Jul 2026 = 18.39; window average about 17.38. The currency isn't labelled; it is probably USD. No Nov 2025 value is shown, and there is no objective or placement split. **(S)** — [Superads UK CPM](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpm-cost-per-mille/united-kingdom)
- **The same Superads data quoted in GBP by a UK agency:** UK median CPM about £12.90 (Jul 2025–Jul 2026); **low about £9.80 in Oct 2025; high about £19.60 in Dec 2025**; UK median CPC £1.18; typical 2026 UK CPC £0.45–£1.50. **(S)** — [Edge Digital, 2026 UK pricing guide](https://www.edgedigital.net/how-much-does-facebook-ads-cost/). Note: 19.60/26.48 = 0.74, which is consistent with Superads being in USD.
- **UK CPM by objective (Edge Digital, 2026, not attributed to a dataset):** awareness/reach **£4–£8 CPM**; leads and sales £15–£30 CPM (lead CPM is 2–3x awareness). It says Q4 lifts CPM 35–60% (unattributed) and advises planning Q4 cost per result about 50% higher. **(S, weak)** — [Edge Digital](https://www.edgedigital.net/how-much-does-facebook-ads-cost/)
- **Other UK ecommerce CPMs:** Lebesgue UK ecommerce CPM **$11.81** (2026, period and method not stated) **(S)** — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/facebook-cpm-by-country). Madgicx UK **$10.85** for 2025 **(S)** — [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/facebook-ads-cost). Vaizle UK **$23.79** for 2025 (older dataset, conflicts with the others) **(S)** — [Vaizle](https://insights.vaizle.com/facebook-ad-benchmarks/)
- **A UK guide warns** that Meta publishes no country-level CPM benchmarks and questions the samples behind many UK tables. **(S)** — [Outra](https://outra.co.uk/blog/facebook-ads-cost)
- **Reach vs Traffic CPM (global, 2026):** Reach about **$7.19** vs Traffic about **$8.94**; "reach and awareness campaigns carry the lowest CPMs". **(S, global)** — [Digital Applied](https://digitalapplied.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026-cpc-cpm-ctr-industry). This conflicts with a US 2025 source (reach $10–$15 vs traffic $8–$15, "not significantly different" but traffic varies more) **(S, US)** — [Affect Group via Creatify](https://creatify.ai/blog/what-is-a-good-cpc-cpm-and-ctr-for-meta-ads). A B2B dataset shows the reverse (Reach $8 CPM / 0.30% CTR vs Traffic $5 CPM / 1.20% CTR) **(S, B2B, likely not transferable)** — [Refine Labs-derived brief](https://demgen-dailyv3.pages.dev/paidlab/q1-2026-b2b-awareness-benchmarks-what-ctr-cpm-and-cpc-really-signal/)
- **The Reach objective spreads delivery evenly across the audience; Traffic buys whatever inventory gets clicks** (practitioner explanation). **(S)** — [LeadEnforce](https://leadenforce.com/blog/why-meta-prefers-some-campaign-objectives-over-others)
- **Frequency caps** are available on Reach (and Awareness) campaigns, set at the ad-set level. In Jon Loomer's test, Meta held close to the cap: average frequency was **1.13**. **(S, practitioner)** — [Jon Loomer, manage frequency](https://www.jonloomer.com/manage-facebook-ad-frequency/); [Perfect Search Media](https://www.perfectsearchmedia.com/blog/master-frequency-caps-facebook-ads)
- **Meta's UK minimum spend** is "just £1 a day". **(S)** — [Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/marketing/facebook-ads-cost/)
- **New-account daily spending limit:** widely reported as about **$50/day** for new accounts, set by Meta and rising with good standing. Business verification may lift it faster. No official Meta page confirming $50 was found. **(S, third-party)** — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/daily-spending-limits-stop-your-ads/); [Coinis](https://coinis.com/blog/new-meta-ad-accounts-the-survival-guide)
- **Ad review:** Meta aims to decide on review requests within 48 hours. Allow at least 24 hours before judging delivery (from the baseline file). **(S)** — [Influee](https://influee.co/ca/blog/facebook-ads-running-but-not-spending)
- **Advantage+ audience without a pixel:** detailed-targeting inputs are "suggestions" Meta can go beyond; location and the age minimum stay hard controls. **(S)** — [Jon Loomer, targeting suggestions](https://www.jonloomer.com/targeting-suggestions/); [Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/metas-advantage-audience/)

### Inferences
- **December 2026 Meta CPM to plan on (E):** all-objective median about **£16–£22** for 2–12 Dec (the Dec 2025 median was about £19.60, and the days after Cyber Monday sit about +20% over baseline). Reach objective about **£6–£12** (the £4–£8 awareness range plus the 35–60% Q4 uplift). Traffic about **£15–£25**.
- **What £150 / £300 buys on Meta (E):**

  | Objective | Dec CPM (E) | £150 impressions | £150 people (freq ~1.2–1.5) | £300 people | Clicks at £300 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Reach + frequency cap 1 per 7 days | £6–£12 | 12,500–25,000 | **~9,000–21,000** | **~18,000–42,000** | ~0.3–0.6% CTR → ~75–250 |
  | Traffic (link clicks) | £15–£25 | 6,000–10,000 | ~3,500–7,000 | ~7,000–14,000 | ~£1.00–£2.00 CPC → ~150–300 |
  | Awareness (ad recall) | about the same as Reach or a little cheaper, but no frequency control | similar | fewer *unique* people than Reach (it re-serves) | — | low |

- **Is Reach + a frequency cap sensible? Yes, if the goal is "most different UK adults see it once or twice".** It is the only objective that buys *unique people* rather than clicks, and the cap stops the budget being spent showing the same grandparent the ad six times. The catch is that Reach finds people who are cheap to show ads to, not people who click. For sales, the creative has to work without a click: the product, the price "from £60", "last order 12 Dec", and an easy code to remember. Expect sales from Reach to be few and mostly unmeasured, because people type the URL later and skip the code.
- **Objective for the most reach that still drives *some* sales without a pixel (E):** Reach with a 1-per-7-days cap gives the most people. Traffic (link clicks) gives countable visits at about a third of the reach. Landing-page-view optimisation may work without a pixel (unclear; see the baseline file). Purchase/Sales objectives are pointless without a pixel.
- **The spend limit won't bind:** £150 over 11 days is about £14/day and £300 is about £27/day, both under the reported ~$50 (£38) new-account limit. Set up by about 28 Nov so review and verification aren't on the critical path.

### Gaps
- No UK CPM split by objective for December from a dataset with a stated method. The £4–£8 awareness range is unattributed.
- No official Meta new-account limit or first-ad review time.
- No Nov 2025 UK value in Superads; no UK Dec 2024 figure found.

## 2. Precision: how much of each audience is parents of 0–10s or grandparents, and Meta's "Parents" segments

### Takeaway
A broad UK 25–65+ audience on any platform is mostly *not* the target: roughly **a quarter to a third** of UK adults 25–65+ are parents of under-11s or grandparents of young children **(E)**. Meta still offers "Parents" demographics by child age in 2026 guides, but they **infer a child's age**. Under the project rule that is a grey area and a **founder decision**, not a default. Contextual options (gift keywords, parenting content) raise precision without any data about children.

### Cited Findings
- **UK families:** 19.9 million families in 2025; in 2024, **42.3% of families had one or more dependent children** (about 8.4 million families). **(S)** — [ONS Families and households 2025](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/families/bulletins/familiesandhouseholds/2025)
- **Meta "Parents" segments still exist in 2026:** Hootsuite (27 Aug 2026) says advertisers "can choose to target parents" or "parents with toddlers" under detailed targeting. It reports no removal of parental options; its only removals are sensitive categories (health, race, politics, religion). **(S)** — [Hootsuite, Facebook ad targeting 2026](https://blog.hootsuite.com/facebook-ad-targeting/). A 2025 guide lists "Parents with toddlers" among the available options. **(S)** — [Adamigo](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/7-tips-for-interest-based-targeting-on-meta-ads). Further 2026 guides list all parents, toddlers, preschoolers, early school age, pre-teens, teens and expecting parents (from the baseline file). **(S, secondary)** — [Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/facebook-detailed-targeting-list-2024/); [LeadEnforce](https://leadenforce.com/blog/facebook-ads-in-2026-what-targeting-will-look-like)
- **What Meta did cut:** detailed-targeting **exclusions** stopped from 31 Mar 2025, and niche interest subcategories were consolidated into broader groups from June 2025. **(S)** — [Adamigo](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/7-tips-for-interest-based-targeting-on-meta-ads); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/408262/meta-further-consolidates-ad-targeting-options.html)
- **Under Advantage+ audience these segments are only suggestions,** so Meta can show the ads to non-parents anyway. **(S)** — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/targeting-suggestions/)
- **Pinterest UK audience** skews towards ages 25–54 (UK agency). **(S, weak)** — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/pinterest-advertising-cost-uk/)
- **Nextdoor UK:** over 10 million verified neighbours; **1 in 4 UK households** use it. **(S)** — [Nextdoor UK press release, 18 Dec 2025](https://about.nextdoor.com/gb/news/nextdoor-announces-new-self-serve-ads-platform-for-small-businesses)
- **Microsoft/Bing audience** over-indexes on older age groups and higher incomes; the largest cohort is 35–54. Bing has about 11.8% of UK desktop search (agency figure). **(S, vendor/agency)** — [Finch](https://finch.com/blog/microsoft-advertising-worth-it-retailers/); [Semetrical](https://www.semetrical.com/microsoft-advertising-vs-google-ads/)

### Inferences
- **Share on target (E), assuming target = parents of 0–10s + grandparents of young children.** Parents of 0–10s are maybe 8–10m UK adults (ONS families × the share with a child under 11 × about 1.7 adults per family, E). Grandparents of young children are maybe a further 6–9m (E, no source found). Against about 40m UK adults aged 25–65+, that gives **~30–45% of 25–65+ adults (E)**. Allow for platform skew:

  | Option | Targeting basis | Est. share on target (E) | Within project rule? |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Meta broad, UK, 25–65+ | adult age only | ~25–35% | Yes (clean default) |
  | Meta broad, two ad sets 25–44 and 55–65+ | adult age only (parent-age and grandparent-age bands) | ~35–45% | Yes |
  | Meta "Parents (All)" suggestion | adult's own parental status (no child age) | ~50–70% (accuracy unknown) | Probably yes; flag to founder |
  | Meta "Parents with toddlers / preschoolers / early school age" | **infers a child's age** | ~60–80% (accuracy unknown) | **Grey area: founder decision, not default** |
  | Pinterest keywords ("personalised gift from child's drawing", "gifts from kids", "gift for grandparents from grandchildren") | the adult's own search context | ~50–70% | Yes (contextual) |
  | Google/Microsoft Shopping & Search on the same terms | the adult's own search | ~60–80% | Yes (contextual) |
  | YouTube placements on UK parenting channels (exclude "made for kids") | content context | ~40–60% | Yes, *if* kids' content is excluded |
  | Nextdoor UK-wide | household / neighbourhood | ~25–35% | Yes |
  | Reddit (r/UKParenting-type communities) | community context | ~50%+ in-community, tiny volume | Yes, but small |

- The Meta parent segments' accuracy is unknown: Meta infers them from behaviour. Under Advantage+ they only nudge delivery. The precision gain is uncertain, and the child-age inference is the part that cuts against "no profiling of children". **Recommendation: do not use the child-age segments by default; ask the founder.** "Parents (All)" is about the adult, not the child, but it is still inferred, so flag it too.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy figures for Meta parental segments in the UK.
- No sourced count of UK grandparents of under-11s. The often-quoted "14 million grandparents" could not be verified.
- No platform publishes its UK share of parents of young children.

## 3. Google: Shopping / Performance Max without a conversion tag, UK gift CPCs, Demand Gen / YouTube CPMs, contextual targeting

### Takeaway
**Standard Shopping** (from the existing Merchant feed) with Maximise Clicks or manual CPC is the sensible no-tag option: you pay only per click, and impressions to people searching for gifts are free. **Performance Max** *can* be created without conversion tracking, but Google says its spend "may be limited", and its bidding needs conversions, so skip it. European Shopping CPCs averaged about **€0.40 in Dec 2025**; UK personalised-gift terms are probably **£0.40–£1.00 (E)**. YouTube is mostly a £5–£25 CPM video buy, which needs video creative and goes poorly with a 2-week flight.

### Cited Findings
- **PMax without tracking:** "You can create Performance Max campaigns without conversion tracking", but the "campaign's spend may be limited" if requirements aren't met. Google's bidding guidance covers only Maximise conversions / conversion value (and tCPA/tROAS). **(S)** — [Google Ads Help: Create a Performance Max campaign](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10724896?hl=en)
- **Shopping CPC, Europe (Smarter Ecommerce, all categories):** Shopping CPC spiked to about **€0.42 in Nov 2025** and settled near **€0.40 in Dec 2025**. **(S, Europe-wide, not UK or gifts)** — [smec Q4 2025 report](https://smarter-ecommerce.com/en/smec-market-observer/reports/Q4_2025/)
- **European ecommerce Google CPC up 15% year on year** (Channable benchmark, 2026). **(S)** — [ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2026/07/cost-of-clicks-rises-15-year-over-year-and-roas-drops-46/); [Iceclog](https://iceclog.com/rising-cost-per-click-put-product-data-under-pressure/)
- **Keyword CPC estimates (third-party tool, market unstated, USD):** "custom gift" about **$0.74** (high competition); "personalised cushion" about **$1.31**. **(S, weak)** — [RankHero custom gift](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/custom-gift); [RankHero personalised cushion](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/personalised-cushion)
- **UK personalised gifting** is growing about 11% a year (+£980m 2025–2029). **(S)** — [Salience 2025 gift analysis](https://salience.co.uk/insight/2025-gift-industry-analysis/)
- **YouTube UK:** skippable in-stream about **£0.01–£0.04 per view**; bumper / non-skippable about **£5–£25 CPM**. **(S, UK agency)** — [Giraffe Social Media](https://www.giraffesocialmedia.co.uk/youtube-ads-explained-a-visual-overview-of-formats-costs-and-key-strategies/)
- **Demand Gen** replaced Video Action Campaigns. The only cost figure found is about $0.50–$2.00 per click (US-leaning); no UK Demand Gen CPM was found. **(S, US)** — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/youtube-ads-cost/); [Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/youtube-ads-complete-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- **Recommended Google setup (E):** a standard Shopping campaign from the existing Merchant feed, UK only, Maximise Clicks with a **max CPC cap of about £0.60–£0.80** (or manual CPC). Use negative keywords for irrelevant terms (e.g. "free", "template", "printable", "diy"). Optionally add a small Search campaign on exact/phrase terms such as "personalised gift from child's drawing", "turn kids drawing into", "gifts from kids for grandparents" and "child's artwork framed". These are contextual (the adult's own search) and fully within the project rule. Measure with UTM-tagged final URLs and a GOOGLE code. Google's own click reports work with no tag.
- **What £100 buys on Shopping (E):** at £0.40–£1.00 CPC, **100–250 clicks**. At a Shopping CTR of roughly 0.7–1.5% (E), that is also about 7,000–35,000 product impressions to people actively searching for gifts. Searchers are closest to buying, so Shopping gives **the best chance of a sale per £** but **the least reach per £**: reach is capped by how many people search those terms in 10 days.
- **YouTube on parenting channels (E):** contextually clean if "made for kids" and children's content are excluded (children's channels can't carry personalised ads anyway). At £5–£25 CPM it is no cheaper than Meta Reach, it needs a video ad, and placement lists take time to build. Not worth it at £150–£400 in December.

### Gaps
- No UK December CPC for "personalised gifts" terms. Check Keyword Planner (free with a Google Ads account; founder's step).
- Whether Maximise Clicks is available for Shopping in the current UI was not re-verified (from knowledge: yes for standard Shopping, no for PMax).

## 4. Pinterest, Nextdoor, Reddit and Microsoft (Bing) Shopping

### Takeaway
**Pinterest** has the lowest published UK CPMs (**£1.50–£4**, year-round) and keyword targeting on gift searches, so it is the cheapest *precise* reach on paper. Its December delivery volume and UK conversion are unproven. **Microsoft Shopping** gets cheaper clicks and an older audience but small volume. **Nextdoor** (UK self-serve since Dec 2025) and **Reddit** are poor fits for a 10-day national push.

### Cited Findings
- **Pinterest UK:** CPM **£1.50–£4**; CPC **£0.10–£1.20** (most retail £0.25–£0.60; consideration 10p–40p); costs "rise sharply" Sept–Nov and again in Dec–Jan (no figures); minimum "a few pounds a day"; test budget £150–£300/month. Based on the author's UK client work (2026), not a dataset. **(S, weak)** — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/pinterest-advertising-cost-uk/)
- **Pinterest minimum bids:** £4.00 CPM / £0.08 CPC (undated). **(S)** — [Strike Social](https://strikesocial.com/blog/pinterest-advertising-cost/). No official minimum budget. **(S)** — [Tailwind](https://www.tailwindapp.com/blog/how-much-does-pinterest-advertising-cost)
- **Pinterest gift intent:** someone searches for a gift every three seconds Oct–Dec. **(S)** — [Pinterest Business UK](https://business.pinterest.com/en-gb/blog/festive-gifting-pinterest/)
- **Nextdoor UK:** self-serve launched 18 Dec 2025; minimum spend "just a few pounds"; targeting by location, demographics and interests; pixel offered; **no CPM/CPC published**. **(S)** — [Nextdoor UK](https://about.nextdoor.com/gb/news/nextdoor-announces-new-self-serve-ads-platform-for-small-businesses). Third-party CPM about $2–$8 (non-UK, weak). **(S, weak)** — [risingsunartscentre guide](https://new.risingsunartscentre.org/news/nextdoor-advertising-cost-price-guide-for-local-reach-2026.html). Nextdoor reported that AI click optimisation cut CPC by 75% in a UK beta. **(S)** — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/nextdoors-ai-click-optimisation-cut-cost-per-click-by-75-in-uk-beta/)
- **Reddit:** **$5/day** minimum, no enforced minimum bid (other sources: $3.50 CPM floor for reach; $50 lifetime minimum); CPM about $2–$5 (wide spread, $0.50–$15; US/UK/CA cost more). **(S, third-party)** — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/reddit-ads-minimum-prices); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/reddit-ads-cost); [Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/reddit-ads/reddit-ads-cost-benchmarks)
- **Microsoft Ads:** CPC typically **20–35% lower** than Google for the same keywords (vendor); US averages $1.54 vs $2.69. **(S, US/vendor)** — [Ecommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/bing-microsoft-ads-for-shopify-the-most-underrated-channel/); [Semetrical](https://www.semetrical.com/microsoft-advertising-vs-google-ads/). Product feeds and campaigns can be imported from Google Ads. **(S)** — [Anicca](https://anicca.co.uk/resources-guides/ecommerce-ppc-microsoft-ads-guide/). Over 14 million UK searchers (agency). **(S, agency)** — [Doublespark](https://www.doublespark.co.uk/cambridge/ppc/bing-ads-microsoft-advertising)

### Inferences
- **Pinterest in Dec (E):** CPM probably **£3–£6** in December (the published £1.50–£4 plus the seasonal rise). £100 then buys about 17,000–33,000 impressions, or **~12,000–25,000 people**, with keyword targeting on the adult's own gift searches (on-target ~50–70%). On cost per 1,000 *on-target* people it is the cheapest option in this table (see section 5). Risks: a new account needs business setup and a site claim (1–3 days); December keyword inventory may not spend the budget; and Pinterest users plan early, so 2–12 Dec catches the tail of the gift-browsing curve.
- **Microsoft Shopping (E):** worth it only as a cheap add-on, because importing the Google campaign takes minutes once Google exists. Volume at these terms will be small (maybe tens of clicks). The older desktop skew does suit grandparents. UK Shopping availability is from knowledge (Microsoft Merchant Center supports the UK) and was not re-verified.
- **Nextdoor (E):** good for one region, not UK-wide. It is new in the UK with no published CPMs, and its pixel-led tools are unusable here. Skip.
- **Reddit (E):** the UK audience skews young and male. Parenting subreddits are tiny at these budgets, and the $5/day minimum on top of review and setup is effort for little reach. Skip.

### Gaps
- No official UK CPM from Pinterest, Nextdoor, Reddit or Microsoft.
- No UK December Pinterest cost figures.
- Microsoft Shopping UK availability and minimums not confirmed on a Microsoft page (the fetch was blocked).

## 5. Comparison table and decisive recommendation

### Takeaway
**Single best channel for maximum UK reach per £ with a reasonable chance of sales: Meta (Facebook + Instagram), Reach objective, UK, ages 25–65+ in two age bands, frequency cap 1 per 7 days, 2–12 Dec (frames/blankets/cushions creative to 14 Dec).** Pair it with a small **Google standard Shopping** campaign from the existing feed as the "sales leg" from £300 up. Pinterest is the best precision-per-£ alternative but a higher-risk first-time setup in December.

### Cited Findings (summary; all figures from sections 1–4)

- Pinterest UK CPM £1.50–£4. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/pinterest-advertising-cost-uk/)

- **Why Meta Reach over Pinterest, even though Pinterest looks cheaper per on-target person:** (1) Meta reaches **grandparents 55–65+** at scale; Pinterest skews women 25–54. (2) It is the fastest and most predictable to spend in an 11-day window, and the frequency cap guarantees *breadth*. (3) Pinterest's December keyword inventory and UK costs are unproven for a first-time account, and an under-delivering campaign wastes the window. If the founder only cares about parents of young children (not grandparents), Pinterest is a fair alternative primary.
- **Why add Google Shopping:** it is the only channel where the person is *already shopping for a gift*. The feed exists, no tag is needed for click bidding, and it pays only per click. It is the most likely to produce code-tracked or UTM-tracked sales.
- **Profit reality (E):** at £10–£14 profit per order, £150 breaks even only at about 11–15 orders and £300 at about 21–30 orders. The baseline file's Meta cost per sale (£45–£110) means **none of these channels is likely to pay back in-season**. Treat paid as buying reach and learning, and cap it.
