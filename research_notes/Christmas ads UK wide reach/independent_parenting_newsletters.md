# Independent UK parenting and family-life email newsletters: sponsorship for a 26 Nov to 4 Dec 2026 send

Research done 18 Nov 2026 using public pages only. Nobody was contacted, nothing was subscribed to, and no forms or media kits were requested. S means published, with a URL. E means my estimate. Subscriber counts are the "Over N subscribers" text on each Substack homepage, read on 18 Nov 2026.

## Which UK-based parenting/family newsletters have the biggest lists (10k+)?

### Takeaway
The biggest independent UK parenting newsletters I could verify have about 11k to 25k subscribers. All four are on Substack: How to Feed (25k+), Talking Sense from Dr Martha (18k+), Every Shade of Grey (16k+) and The Frazzled Mums Club (11k+). I found no independent UK parenting or family-lifestyle newsletter with a UK list over 50k. Substack's Parenting charts are led by US writers.

### Cited Findings
- Substack's "Top Parenting" chart lists 25 publications. The leaders are US ("Hundreds of thousands" for Emily Ley and Big Little Feelings). The UK-linked entries are "Talking Sense from Dr Martha Psychologist" and "Every Shade of Grey" (Katherine Ormerod), both shown as "Tens of thousands". "Anchor Baby" (Rosemary Mac Cabe) is Irish — [Substack top parenting](https://substack.com/top/parenting)
- Reletter's free Parenting chart (top 250) also includes these UK/Irish entries: How to Feed by Charlotte Stirling-Reed (#42), Every Shade of Grey (#37), Talking Sense (#32), The Mother Project with Sophie Beresiner (#103), The Candy Club with Lorraine Candy (#110), Gentle Parenting with Sarah Ockwell-Smith (#166), Stella O'Malley (#95, Irish) and The Unravelling with Caroline Foran (#98, Irish) — [Reletter Parenting chart](https://reletter.com/charts/substack/parenting/free)
- **How to Feed (Charlotte Stirling-Reed, "The Baby and Child Nutritionist"): "Over 25,000 subscribers"** (S, 18 Nov 2026) — [charlottestirlingreed.substack.com](https://charlottestirlingreed.substack.com/). Weekly, about 6 months old, "Hundreds of paid subscribers". It offers practical parenting scripts from a clinical psychologist specialising in family and child behaviour — [Reletter listing](https://reletter.com/publications/talking-sense-from-dr-martha-psychologist)
- **Every Shade of Grey (Katherine Ormerod): "Over 16,000 subscribers"** (S, 18 Nov 2026) — [katherineormerod.substack.com](https://katherineormerod.substack.com/). The tagline is "Style, Beauty, Home, Gender Politics, Digital Culture & Parental Survivalism". The paid tier is £5 a month — [About page](https://katherineormerod.substack.com/about). It publishes twice weekly and the author is a former Grazia/Glamour/Sunday Times Style journalist — [Reletter listing](https://reletter.com/publications/every-shade-of-grey)

### Inferences
- UK independent parenting newsletters are small next to the US ones. Nothing UK-based I found tops 25k, and none is close to a 50k UK list.
- How to Feed is the best fit for Snickerdoodle's core buyer, parents of babies to under-11s. Every Shade of Grey is the best fit for the "lifestyle" brief (style, home, gifting), but fewer of its readers have young children.
- The Candy Club and How to Raise a Teen are written for parents of teens. Snickerdoodle sells keepsakes of children's drawings, so these are weak fits, and The Candy Club is also under 10k.

### Gaps
- Exact UK share of each list. No newsletter publishes a geographic split, and the Reletter fields are gated.
- Whether any beehiiv/Kit/Ghost UK parenting newsletter is over 10k. Searches turned up no UK parenting titles on Passionfroot, and I did not find beehiiv ad-network or SparkLoop UK parenting listings.
- I could not check Mother Pukka, Unmumsy Mum, Giovanna Fletcher or Clemmie Telford for newsletter lists within the search budget. None surfaced on the Substack or Reletter parenting charts.

## For each: audience, lifestyle focus, sponsorship/affiliate acceptance, published price, booking route, lead time, format

### Takeaway
None of the four UK newsletters over 10k publishes a sponsorship price, a media kit or a sponsor page. None showed past sponsored placements either. Every one is "ask", and the only public contact routes I found were Instagram or Substack itself (no generic business email on the pages I read).

### Cited Findings
- **How to Feed:** for parents feeding babies, toddlers, children and the whole family. Reletter has fields for "Accepts Sponsors" and "estimated cost per ad", but both are behind a paywall, and it advises asking the author for a media kit. The homepage mentions no sponsors, partners or affiliates — [Reletter](https://reletter.com/publications/charlottes-substack-1395); [homepage](https://charlottestirlingreed.substack.com/)
- **Talking Sense (Dr Martha):** for "Parents and Carers"; children's ages are not stated. The pages mention no sponsorship, affiliates or business contact — [About](https://drmarthapsychologist.substack.com/about)
- **Every Shade of Grey:** covers style, beauty, home and parenting. The pages mention no sponsors, gift guides, affiliates or business contact. Reletter says to contact the newsletter for a media kit — [About](https://katherineormerod.substack.com/about); [Reletter](https://reletter.com/publications/every-shade-of-grey)
- **The Frazzled Mums Club (Anna Mathur):** for mums. It doesn't mention sponsorship, but the About page's Amazon book link carries an affiliate tag ("tag=mamasscrapboo-21"), and every Friday free email carries a discount code for her own resources. Contact routes are Instagram @annamathur and annamathur.com — [About](https://annamathur.substack.com/about)
- **The Candy Club:** has a Bookshop.org link with an affiliate-style parameter (aid=16389). It doesn't mention sponsorship or gift guides, and the only contact route is Instagram — [About](https://lorrainecandy.substack.com/about)
- Substack's own sponsorship tools are invite-led and opt-in for publishers. One guide says they are for Bestsellers only, with 100+ paid subscribers, so most deals are direct — [Paved guide](https://www.paved.com/blog/substack-advertising); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/substack-advertising/). A 2024 report says Substack writers keep 100% of partnership revenue (dated) — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/were-all-just-fumbling-around-advertising-on-substack-remains-a-wild-west-for-brands/)
- For a price benchmark on a marketplace (not UK): Passionfroot's "Raising Empowered Kids" parenting newsletter (readers in Canada, Europe and US) charges $1,000 for a headline sponsor issue, $2,500 for three issues and $600 for three classifieds. A general creator newsletter with UK readers lists a €300 top ad and a €100 classified — [Passionfroot REK](https://www.passionfroot.me/rek); [search summary of Passionfroot listings](https://www.passionfroot.me/passionfroot)
- Other UK parenting outlets that do sell ads are not independent creator newsletters. They are enquiry-only, with no prices: The Green Parent (2024 media kit), The Parents' Guide To ("600,000+ readers"), and Parentkind, whose newsletter goes to 20k PTA subscribers with a 30%+ open rate and has a narrow PTA audience — [Green Parent](https://thegreenparent.co.uk/advertise/); [Parents' Guide To](https://www.theparentsguideto.co.uk/advertise-with-us); [Parentkind](https://www.parentkind.org.uk/about-us/advertising-and-sponsorship-opportunities)

### Inferences
- Lead time (E): these are one-person newsletters with no ad inventory system, so a deal is a personal yes/no. For a 26 Nov to 4 Dec send, the ask would need to go out by about 20 to 21 Nov, and many will already have their Christmas content planned.
- Creative format (E): the likely format is a short native mention or "things I'm loving" / gift-list item (about 50 to 100 words, an image and a link) written in the creator's voice, not a banner. On Substack, a code in a gift list is the most natural fit.

### Gaps
- No published price, open rate, lead time or format for any UK independent parenting newsletter. All need a media-kit request, which was outside this brief.
- Generic business emails were not visible on the pages I fetched. Agents and management may be listed elsewhere, such as on Instagram bios, but I did not check.

## Cost per 1,000 subscribers / opens / on-target opens

### Takeaway
No prices are published, so I modelled each newsletter at the £300 budget cap (E) with a 40% open rate (E). On that basis How to Feed is by far the cheapest per on-target open, at about £51 per 1,000. Break-even at £10 to £14 profit per order needs 21 to 30 orders, and that is a stretch from 2,000 to 6,000 on-target opens.

### Cited Findings
- List sizes are as cited above: 25k / 18k / 16k / 11k / 4k (S, 18 Nov 2026, Substack homepages).
- None publishes an open rate, so the 40% E default from the brief applies.

### Inferences
Model, all at an assumed £300 price (E, not quoted). On-target means UK parents of under-11s (E):

| Newsletter | Subs (S) | Opens @40% (E) | UK share (E) | Under-11 parent share (E) | On-target opens (E) | £/1k subs | £/1k opens | £/1k on-target opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| How to Feed (Stirling-Reed) | 25,000 | 10,000 | 65% | 90% (weaning/toddler/child feeding) | 5,850 | £12.00 | £30.00 | **£51** |
| Talking Sense (Dr Martha) | 18,000 | 7,200 | 60% | 85% | 3,670 | £16.67 | £41.67 | **£82** |
| Frazzled Mums Club (Mathur) | 11,000 | 4,400 | 80% | 80% | 2,820 | £27.27 | £68.18 | **£106** |
| Every Shade of Grey (Ormerod) | 16,000 | 6,400 | 75% | 45% (lifestyle readers, not all parents) | 2,160 | £18.75 | £46.88 | **£139** |
| The Candy Club (Candy) | 4,000 | 1,600 | 80% | ~10% (teens 12–25) | ~130 | £75.00 | £187.50 | **~£2,300** |

- The UK-share estimates are inferences. All four writers are UK-based (British spelling and references, GBP pricing, BACP accreditation), but their Instagram-fed audiences are international.
- Break-even: £300 ÷ £12 ≈ 25 orders. For How to Feed that means 25 / 5,850 ≈ 0.43% of on-target opens buying a £60 to £65+ item. That is optimistic for a single mention, so a fixed fee is likely to lose money unless it is well under £300.

### Gaps
- Real quotes may be well above £300. The only parenting marketplace benchmark I found is $1,000 per headline issue on a US/CA/EU list of unstated size, which points to UK creators asking more than this model assumes.

## Which are most likely to accept a commission-only (10% of pre-VAT order) deal?

### Takeaway
Anna Mathur and Katherine Ormerod look the most open to affiliate or commission links: Mathur already uses affiliate tags and discount codes, and Ormerod writes a style/home gift-pick format. Dr Martha (clinical psychologist) and Charlotte Stirling-Reed (nutritionist) are less likely to plug a non-category product commission-only, though Stirling-Reed's audience fits best.

### Cited Findings
- Mathur's page uses an Amazon affiliate tag and a weekly discount-code slot — [About](https://annamathur.substack.com/about)
- The Candy Club's page uses a Bookshop.org affiliate-style link — [About](https://lorrainecandy.substack.com/about)
- Every Shade of Grey covers style, beauty and home, and the author is a fashion journalist — [About](https://katherineormerod.substack.com/about); [Reletter](https://reletter.com/publications/every-shade-of-grey)

### Inferences
- Likelihood of yes to commission-only (E): Mathur, then Ormerod, then Stirling-Reed, then Dr Martha. 10% of a £65 frame (about £5.40 before VAT) is small per sale, so the pitch should be a reader code plus 10%, framed as a gift idea.
- A psychologist or nutritionist may avoid non-expertise product endorsements. That is a professional-positioning inference, not a cited fact.

### Gaps
- None of them states an affiliate or commission policy publicly.

## Ranked top 5 by cost per 1,000 on-target opens

### Takeaway
None of the five publishes a price, so every row below is "ask". None has a UK list over 50k. Ranked at an assumed £300 (E):

1. **How to Feed, Charlotte Stirling-Reed.** 25k+ (S). About £51 per 1k on-target opens (E). Ask; contact via Substack/Instagram. Best audience fit.
2. **Talking Sense, Dr Martha Deiros Collado.** 18k+ (S). About £82 (E). Ask.
3. **The Frazzled Mums Club, Anna Mathur.** 11k+ (S). About £106 (E). Ask. The **most likely to accept commission-only**.
4. **Every Shade of Grey, Katherine Ormerod.** 16k+ (S). About £139 (E). Ask. **Best lifestyle and gift-list fit** and second most likely for commission.
5. **The Candy Club, Lorraine Candy.** 4k+ (S). About £2,300 (E). Ask. A teen-parent audience, so the weakest fit; it is listed only because it is UK and a known creator.

### Cited Findings
- All counts are from the Substack homepages cited above (18 Nov 2026). There were no published prices: [How to Feed](https://charlottestirlingreed.substack.com/), [Talking Sense](https://drmarthapsychologist.substack.com/), [Frazzled Mums Club](https://annamathur.substack.com/), [Every Shade of Grey](https://katherineormerod.substack.com/), [Candy Club](https://lorrainecandy.substack.com/)

### Inferences
- **Recommendation:** offer commission-only (10% plus a reader code) to Mathur and Ormerod first, and How to Feed alongside. Only pay a flat fee if it is ≤£150, which halves the break-even to about 12 orders, and only for How to Feed. At £300, none of these lists is likely to pay back on £10 to £14 profit per order.

### Gaps
- Published prices: none found for any of the five.
- Open rates and UK splits: unpublished; all modelled (E).
