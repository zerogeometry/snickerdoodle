# Photo-printing subscriptions, photo-book memberships, greetings-card memberships and cloud photo storage — UK benchmarks (researched 26 Nov 2026)

Scope note: researched via web search + direct fetch of official pages where possible. Where only a third-party or undated source was available it is flagged. Prices are GBP incl. VAT unless stated. "(stale?)" = source undated or older than 12 months.

## Q1. Service-by-service fact sheet: exact UK price points and what's bundled

### Takeaway
UK families face three distinct price anchors: (a) cloud storage at roughly £1–£9/month (iCloud+ £0.99–£8.99 for 50GB–2TB; Google One £1.59 for 100GB, £7.99 for 2TB), (b) "free prints, pay postage" apps with NO subscription fee (FreePrints, Photobox app, Snapfish app — the monthly allowance is a retention hook, revenue comes from postage and upsell), and (c) small annual discount memberships in greetings cards at ~£10/year (Moonpig Plus £10.99/yr, XTRAthortful £9.99/yr). True paid print/book subscriptions (Chatbooks, Timeshel, Google Premium Print Series) are US-centric and largely not sold in the UK.

### Inferences
- In the UK, no mainstream print brand charges a monthly subscription; the "free monthly allowance + postage" model has set expectations that a monthly print entitlement costs roughly £1.49–£4.49 per month (postage only).
- Print companies treat storage as a free loss-leader tied to an 18-month purchase rule (Photobox, Shutterfly) — a "keep your keepsakes forever" promise would be a genuine differentiator but also a cost liability.
- The ~£10/year card membership (Moonpig, thortful) is the closest direct analogue for Snickerdoodle's card line; both are annual, discount-led (30–33%), and auto-renewing.

## Q2. Which models clearly work vs failed

### Takeaway
The clear UK winner is the cheap annual discount membership bolted onto a high-frequency purchase (Moonpig Plus: 1m+ members, +36% YoY, "strong renewal behaviour"). "Free prints, pay postage" (FreePrints/PlanetArt) also works at scale because postage covers cost and upsells carry margin. Automatic monthly print/book subscriptions have a poor track record (Groovebook closed 2022, Google's print subscription closed after 4 months in 2020, Lifecake closed) and "unlimited free storage" has repeatedly been withdrawn (Shutterfly 2023, Amazon 2016).

### Cited Findings
- Moonpig Group FY26 H1 (six months to 31 Oct 2025, published 9 Dec 2025): "Plus subscribers grew 36% to one million"; "one million subscription members" help generate "over 90% of our revenue from existing customers"; Plus delivers "clear uplifts in order frequency, attach rates, and strong renewal behaviour"; card revenue +9.7% partly from "the scaling of our Plus subscription base"; purchase frequency 2.95 orders per active customer (LTM); 12.1m active customers; Moonpig adjusted EBITDA margin 30% — [Moonpig Group FY26 H1 results presentation script (PDF)](https://www.moonpig.group/media/z24pxi24/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-half-year-results-presentation-script-final.pdf).
  - Implied: ~1m members × £10.99 ≈ £11m/yr in fees (inference, not stated) — value comes mainly from frequency uplift rather than fee revenue.
- FreePrints economics: owned by PlanetArt (Claranova group); revenue from postage (£1.49–£3.99 UK) and paid sizes/photobooks/gifts; PlanetArt revenue €366m in 2021; author infers postage alone may be profitable as prints ship in letter envelopes — [ProductMint](https://productmint.com/?p=7838) (secondary; inference flagged by author; revenue figure 2021, stale).
- FreePrints UK Trustpilot has ~179,000 reviews (scale signal) — [Trustpilot UK FreePrints](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/freeprintsnow.com?page=9).
- Groovebook (US): auto-printed monthly book of up to 100 phone photos for $2.99/month; Shutterfly acquired it in 2014 for $14.5m; all services ended 8 April 2022, reasons undisclosed — [TechCrunch (acquisition)](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1084104); [Looper](https://www.looper.com/1268989/whatev…) (secondary for closure date).
- Google Photos monthly prints subscription (~$7.99/mo, 10 prints) shut after ~4 months in June 2020, then relaunched at $6.99 — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/google-photos-printing-subscription-is-closing-after-just-four-months-19625517); [TechCrunch Oct 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/20/google-photos-revives-its-prints-subscription-service-expands-same-day-print-options).
- Amazon killed its Unlimited (all-files) storage plan in 2016 and pivoted to prints — [Imaging Resource, Sep 2016](https://old.imaging-resource.com/news/2016/09/24/less-photo-storage-more-prints-amazon-kills-unlimited-photos-plan).
- Shutterfly 2023 storage restriction triggered press backlash and a deadline extension — [NBC Boston/Connecticut](https://www.nbcconnecticut.com/news/national-international/shutterfly-extends-access-to-photos-after-shocked-user-reaches-out-to-nbc10-boston/3018214/).
- Canon image.canon lost some users' original photos/videos in July 2020 (10GB long-term storage) and couldn't restore full-size images — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/canon-says-its-cloud-service-cant-restore-users-lost-videos-or-full-size-images); Canon closed Irista on 31 Jan 2020 — [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/uk/news/canon-kills-off-its-irista-cloud-storage-service).
- Lifecake closure / BackThen migration with complaints about no warning and refunds — [BackThen App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1505173822).
- Chatbooks survives with a monthly mini-book model in US/CA/AU/NZ (not UK) — [Chatbooks help](https://help.chatbooks.com/en/articles/7862721-what-are-monthly-minis) (via search summary). Timeshel has persisted since 2013 (price rise in 2023) — [Timeshel blog](https://philip-anema-5djg.squarespace.com/blog/2023/10/16/new-print-format-price-change).

### Inferences
- Successful models give value on purchases the customer would make anyway (discount on cards) or remove friction while charging only cost-covering postage; failures auto-ship something on a cadence the family doesn't control, or promise "unlimited forever" storage that later gets clawed back.
- For a children's-keepsake brand, a storage withdrawal or shutdown (Lifecake, Shutterfly, Canon) is the most reputation-damaging failure mode; any subscription that holds originals needs a credible exit/download path.
- Chatbooks not serving the UK leaves a gap for a UK monthly mini-book, but Groovebook/Google evidence says low-price auto-books are fragile.

### Gaps
- No published retention/renewal rate for Moonpig Plus (only "strong renewal behaviour").
- No financials for FreePrints UK specifically; no Chatbooks subscriber/churn data found.
- Official Shutterfly statement on Groovebook closure not found.

## Q3. Cancellation and dark-pattern complaints; regulator action

### Takeaway
The big enforcement cases are Amazon Prime (EU 2022 two-click cancellation, applied in UK too; US FTC $2.5bn settlement Sept 2025). No CMA/ASA action against Moonpig Plus or thortful was found, but Moonpig Plus does attract "charged again after cancelling/renewed without realising" Trustpilot complaints. The UK DMCC Act subscription regime (reminders, two cooling-off windows, easy exit) is due in force January 2027 per DWF (Reed Smith says spring 2027) and will cover annual memberships and physical-goods subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- FTC v Amazon (Sept 2025): $1bn civil penalty + $1.5bn refunds over allegations Amazon enrolled customers in Prime without consent and made cancellation difficult; no admission of wrongdoing; order lasts 10 years; cancellation "must not be difficult, costly, confusing or time consuming" — [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/amazon-to-pay-2-5-billion-to-settle-ftc-allegations-it-duped-customers-into-enrolling-in-prime); [Fortune, 25 Sep 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/09/25/amazon-ftc-settlement-prime-automatic-renewal-lina-khan-dark-patterns/).

- DMCC Act subscription contracts regime: in force January 2027 per DWF (Oct 2026) — [DWF](https://dwfgroup.com/ar-ae/news-and-insights/insights/2026/10/dmcca-subscription-contracts-regime-preparing-for-the-new-restrictions); CONFLICT: Reed Smith reported "Spring 2027" — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102moxp/enforcement-delay-and-waiver-dismay-dmcc-subscription-rules-postponed-again/). DBT consultation response published 2 April 2026 — (search summary; see [GOV.UK consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime/consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime-web-accessible-version)); guidance — [Subscription contracts rules guidance (GOV.UK PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6acf97da2de3184ccf8b814e/subscription-contracts-rules-guidance.pdf).
- DMCC obligations: pre-contract info; renewal reminders; 14-day cooling-off at start AND 14 days after each renewal becomes payable; reminders every 6 months for subs without a concessionary period, plus an extra reminder before 12-month+ renewals; notices on a durable medium (email/PDF/SMS); easy exit; recurring product deliveries in scope; only narrow exemptions (e.g. certain charitable heritage memberships) — [DWF](https://dwfgroup.com/ar-ae/news-and-insights/insights/2026/10/dmcca-subscription-contracts-regime-preparing-for-the-new-restrictions); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/cancel-culture-uk-subscription-contracts-regime-takes-shape) (via search summary).

### Inferences
- Any Snickerdoodle subscription launching in 2027 must be DMCC-compliant from day one: online one-step exit, reminders before every renewal, and renewal cooling-off — this makes annual-auto-renew plans slightly more costly to operate and favours transparent designs.
- Moonpig's model (cancel in My Account, reminders 14 and 3 days pre-renewal, partial refund minus used benefits) is a good compliant template; Chatbooks-style expiring credits and app-only pause are exactly the friction to avoid given the brand's "trust shown through control" posture.

### Gaps
- No ASA ruling or CMA action specifically on Moonpig Plus, thortful, FreePrints, Photobox or Chatbooks found.
- Exact DMCC commencement statutory instrument not confirmed (January vs spring 2027 conflict).

A monthly fee for prints in the UK has no strong mainstream precedent.

## Q5. Churn benchmarks: physical print subscriptions vs digital storage

### Takeaway
Recurly's widely cited medians put physical-goods subscription monthly churn at ~10–10.6% versus ~6.6–8.2% for digital; "box of the month" is the highest-churn category. No print-specific or UK-specific churn data was found.

### Cited Findings
- Recurly benchmark (as quoted): overall median 7.02%, digital median 6.63%, physical median 10.11% monthly churn — [Recurly benchmarks for subscription ecommerce](https://recurly.com/research/benchmarks-for-subscription-ecommerce) (direct fetch of this page did NOT show these figures; they come from the search-result snippet/older version — treat with caution); [MarketingCharts](https://dev.marketingcharts.com/industries/retail-and-e-commerce-109475).
- Older Recurly finding: physical-goods subscriptions 10.6% vs all-digital 8.2% churn — [PYMNTS, 2016](https://www.pymnts.com/consumer-insights/2016/recurly-subscription-retail-consumer-insights/) (stale).
- 2026 secondary: physical 10.54% vs digital 6.63%, attributed to shipping friction and cadence mismatch — [eightx DTC subscription churn index 2026](https://eightx.co/blog/dtc-subscription-churn-index-2026) (secondary blog).
- Recurly billing metrics report: SaaS lowest median churn; "Box of the Month" highest — [Recurly annual subscription billing metrics report](https://recurly.com/research/annual-subscription-billling-metrics-report) (via search summary).
- Recurly cross-industry 3.27% (2.41% voluntary + 0.86% involuntary), weighted by B2B software; B2C consumer goods ~6.5%/month — cited by [Digital Applied, Jun 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ecommerce-subscription-retention-playbook-2026-churn-reduction) (secondary).
- Curated boxes 8–12% monthly churn (unsourced range in a blog) — (search summary; treat as anecdotal).
- Annual-plan analogue: Moonpig reports "strong renewal behaviour" on an annual £10.99 plan with 36% member growth, but no rate — [Moonpig FY26 H1 script](https://www.moonpig.group/media/z24pxi24/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-half-year-results-presentation-script-final.pdf).

### Inferences
- At ~10%/month, a physical monthly subscription loses roughly two-thirds of a cohort within a year (1−0.9^12 ≈ 72%) — an arithmetic inference, not a measured figure for print.
- Annual billing and storage-type value (where cancelling means losing access to kept items) tend to lower churn; for a children's-keepsake product, using "lose your keepsakes" as a retention lever would conflict with the brand's privacy and deletion posture, so retention must come from ongoing value.
