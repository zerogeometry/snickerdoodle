# Paid social baseline for Snickerdoodle, Christmas 2026 (Meta, Pinterest, TikTok)

Researched 18 Nov 2026. Currency: $1 = £0.77 throughout (assumption set by the brief). Ad window assumed **25 Nov to 12 Dec 2026 = 18 days**. Unit economics from the brief: order about £35, margin about £5 an order, so **break-even cost per sale is about £5** (before any repeat-purchase value). Earlier-research figures given in the brief and not re-researched: UK Meta CPM about £10 in Oct and about £20 in Dec; a new shop should plan on £45–£110 per Meta sale (mid £55); TikTok $50/day campaign minimum; Pinterest clicks £0.25–£0.60.

Source quality note: about 14 searches/fetches were made. Most results were third-party blogs and benchmark tools rather than official help-centre pages, because Meta's help pages return no body text to the fetch tool. Where a figure is from a blog it is labelled as such.

## 1. Realistic December costs: CPM, CPC, cost per sale, and the Black Friday / Cyber Monday spike

### Takeaway
2025 data shows Meta CPMs about 59% above September in Black Friday week (peaking +84% on Black Friday itself), falling back fast after Cyber Monday but still about 20% above baseline on 3 Dec. For Snickerdoodle, a realistic Meta cost per sale of £45–£110 against £5 margin means paid social will **lose money on every first order**. It is a baseline for comparison, not a profit channel.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 Meta data from the "biggest Meta spenders" on Lebesgue ($22.6m spend, Sep–Nov 2025; region not stated, US holiday timing suggests US-led): median CPM vs September was within 7% until early Nov; +13% on 10 Nov; +27% on 20 Nov; +53% Thanksgiving (27 Nov); **+84% Black Friday (28 Nov)**; +71% Cyber Monday (1 Dec); +48% 2 Dec; **+20% 3 Dec**. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)
- Same source: Black Friday week median CPM +59% (middle half of brands +59% to +116% on Black Friday), Cyber week +31%; 90% of brands paid at least 10% more in BF week. In 2024 the same brands saw +80% BF week and +58% Cyber week, and the ramp began 14 Oct 2024 vs 10 Nov 2025. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)
- Same source: in BF week, conversion rate (purchases per link click) rose +48%, cost per purchase fell −20%, Meta-reported ROAS rose +19%, so higher CPMs were partly offset by higher buying intent. Category CPM rise in BF week: home & decor +116%, other accessories & home goods +23%. Advice: judge post-BFCM campaigns on actual acquisition cost; don't cut on CPM alone. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/are-meta-ads-worth-it-black-friday)
- Supermetrics (515 retail advertisers, Meta, 2025): sector CPMs 22–57% above September by Black Friday week; smallest rise personal care (22%). — [Supermetrics](https://supermetrics.com/ebook/black-friday-retail-playbook)
- GeistM Q4 2025: CPMs up 7% in late October and 11% at the start of December (smaller, fragmented figures). — [GeistM](https://geistm.com/blog/meta-rate-trends-q4-2025/)
- Superads (UK benchmark tool): GB brand-awareness CPC ran about 48% above the global benchmark in Dec 2025; UK cost per purchase is reported as about 96% above global in December (year unclear); late December typically has peak CPMs. — [Superads UK CPC](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpc-cost-per-click/united-kingdom/brand-awareness); [Superads UK cost per purchase](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-purchase/united-kingdom); [Superads UK e-commerce CPP](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-purchase/e-commerce/united-kingdom)
- Pinterest UK (UK blog, 2026): clicks 10p to £1.20; CPM around £1.50–£4; can test on £150–£300 a month. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/pinterest-advertising-cost-uk/)
- Pinterest GBP minimum bids: £0.08 CPC / £0.08 CPE / £4.00 CPM (lowest bids allowed, not budgets). — [Strike Social](https://strikesocial.com/?p=384)

### Inferences
- **Timing for 2026**: Black Friday is 27 Nov 2026 and Cyber Monday 30 Nov 2026. Applying the 2025 curve, a 25 Nov start lands in the most expensive week of the year (roughly +50% to +85% on the worst days). From about 2–3 Dec, CPMs should fall back towards about +20% above baseline. Snickerdoodle's strongest window by cost is probably **2–10 Dec**, which is also when gift deadlines create urgency (cards 10 Dec, most gifts 12 Dec). This is an inference from 2025 US-leaning data.
- **Meta arithmetic (inference)**: at a £20 Dec CPM and an assumed 1.0–1.5% link CTR for cold traffic, CPC = £1.33–£2.00. £300 buys about 15,000 impressions, so roughly 150–225 clicks; £1,000 buys about 50,000 impressions, so roughly 500–750 clicks. At a 1–2% cold conversion rate that is about 2–4 sales for £300 and 5–15 for £1,000. The brief's £45–£110 per sale (mid £55) gives **£300 → 3–7 sales (mid ~5); £1,000 → 9–22 sales (mid ~18)**. At £5 margin, £1,000 returns about £45–£110 of margin, a net loss of about £890–£955 before any repeat value.
- **Pinterest arithmetic (inference)**: at the brief's £0.25–£0.60 CPC, £300 buys 500–1,200 clicks and £1,000 buys 1,670–4,000 clicks. Pinterest traffic is browse-heavy. Assuming a 0.3–1% conversion rate (assumption, no 2026 UK source found), that gives about **£300 → 2–12 sales; £1,000 → 5–40 sales**, so roughly £25–£150 per sale. Wide range, low confidence.
- **TikTok arithmetic (inference)**: the Ads Manager floor is $50/day per campaign = **£38.50/day**, so 18 days costs at least **£693**. A £300 test can run only about 7–8 days in Ads Manager (one campaign, one ad group at the $20 = £15.40 ad-group floor still sits inside the $50 campaign floor). Promote, at $3–5/day (£2.30–£3.85), could cover 18 days for about £40–£70. No reliable 2025/26 UK TikTok CPC or purchase-cost figure was found (see Gaps), so sales are not estimated. Expect TikTok cold traffic to convert no better than Meta, likely worse, for a £35 personalised product bought by parents and grandparents.

### Gaps
- No UK-specific Dec 2025 Meta CPM figure in GBP was found. The £20 Dec CPM from earlier research stands.
- No reliable UK TikTok CPC/CPM/CPA for 2025–26 was fetched. Blog pages exist (e.g. [Influee GB](https://influee.co/gb/blog/tiktok-ads-cost), [The Good Marketer](https://thegoodmarketer.co.uk/blog/how-much-do-tiktok-ads-cost/)) but were not verified.
- No source gives Pinterest conversion rates for UK personalised gifts.

## 2. How fast each platform can start (accounts, verification, review, spending limits, minimum budgets)

### Takeaway
Meta is the fastest to start (same day to 48 hours) but new accounts carry daily spending limits that lift as trust builds. Pinterest has no official minimum spend. TikTok Ads Manager has hard minimums ($50/day campaign, $20/day ad group) that make it a poor fit for a £300 test, while TikTok Promote is a cheap in-app boost.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok official: "For campaigns, both daily budgets and total budgets must exceed 50 USD." "For ad groups, daily budgets must exceed 20 USD." (= £38.50 and £15.40 at $1 = £0.77). — [TikTok Ads help: About daily budgets](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-daily-budgets)
- TikTok Promote (in-app boost of an existing post): minimum about $3–$5/day; runs 1–7 days (third-party sources, not official). — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-promotion/); [megadigital.ai](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/tiktok-promotion-features/)
- One UK guide gives a TikTok campaign minimum of "approximately £20 (£50 lifetime)", which conflicts with TikTok's own USD figures. Treat it as unreliable. — [ProfileTree](https://profiletree.com/tiktok-advertising-guide-for-marketers/)
- Pinterest: "no official minimum daily spend"; Tailwind suggests $5–10/day or $50–100/week for testing. — [Tailwind](https://www.tailwindapp.com/blog/how-much-does-pinterest-advertising-cost). A UK blog suggests £10–£50/day to see results. — [The Good Marketer](https://thegoodmarketer.co.uk/blog/how-much-do-pinterest-ads-cost/)
- Meta minimums (third-party, citing Meta help 203183363050448): as little as $1/day for impression-optimised campaigns; technical minimums "$1–$5 depending on the setup"; Meta can spend up to 75% over the daily budget on a day but no more than 7x daily budget per week (Meta help 190490051321426); daily budget should be at least 5x target cost per result. — [Vizup](https://www.tryvizup.com/blog/meta-ads-minimum-budget-requirements-2026)
- Meta learning phase: about 50 optimisation events per ad set per week (Meta help 112167992830700, cited by third party). — [Vizup](https://www.tryvizup.com/blog/meta-ads-minimum-budget-requirements-2026)
- Meta new-account daily spending limits apply to new business accounts, ad accounts, pages and newly funded accounts, and lift gradually as trust builds. Business verification may raise limits faster. — [Jon Loomer glossary](https://www.jonloomer.com/glossary/daily-spending-limit/); [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/daily-spending-limits-stop-your-ads/)
- Meta review: Meta aims to decide on review requests within 48 hours (rejected-ad appeals). Allow new campaigns at least 24 hours before judging delivery; duplicating a stuck ad can trigger a fresh review. — [Influee](https://influee.co/ca/blog/facebook-ads-running-but-not-spending); [Marketian](https://www.marketian.io/blog/why-facebook-ads-not-spending-money)

### Inferences
- **Meta learning phase is out of reach**: 50 purchases a week at £55 each is about £2,750/week per ad set. A £300–£1,000 test will never leave learning. Optimising for purchases also needs a pixel or CAPI (see section 3), so a no-pixel test would optimise for link clicks or landing-page views. The 5x rule (5 × £55 = £275/day) confirms that purchase optimisation does not fit this budget.
- **Meta daily spending limits** on a brand-new account are unlikely to bind at £17–£55/day (£300–£1,000 over 18 days), but this is unverified. Set up a few days early (by about 22–24 Nov) so verification and the first review are not on the critical path.
- **Public start routes** (no accounts created during this research): Meta Ads Manager https://adsmanager.facebook.com/ ; Meta Business Suite https://business.facebook.com/ ; Pinterest business account https://business.pinterest.com/ then Ads Manager https://ads.pinterest.com/ ; TikTok Ads Manager https://ads.tiktok.com/ (Promote is inside the TikTok app, on a post). Reaching these URLs directly is standard knowledge; they were not fetched.
- **Start-speed ranking (inference)**: Meta boost/Ads Manager, same day to 2 days; TikTok Promote, same day if a business/creator account with suitable posts exists; Pinterest, 1–3 days (business account, website claim helps); TikTok Ads Manager, 1–3 days, but £38.50/day minimum.

### Gaps
- Meta's official minimum-budget table in GBP was not retrievable (the help page returned only a title). The commonly cited Meta rule of thumb (about $1/day impressions, about $5/day clicks, about $40/day low-frequency events) is from memory and **not verified**. Check in Ads Manager.
- No official current Meta ad review SLA for first-time ads, and no official new-account spending-limit figure.
- No official Pinterest or TikTok review times found.

## 3. Measuring sales WITHOUT a pixel, and the consent position (PECR / UK GDPR / ICO)

### Takeaway
Under the ICO's storage-and-access guidance, any advertising pixel or tag needs prior opt-in consent and must not fire before agreement. The Conversions API does not escape consent. With an essential-only banner and children's data, the cleanest route is **no pixel**: run link-click or landing-page-view campaigns, and attribute sales with UTM links plus per-platform discount codes counted server-side.

### Cited Findings
- ICO guidance on "storage and access technologies" replaces the 2019 cookie guidance and covers tracking pixels, link decoration, local storage, fingerprinting, scripts and tags. Using them for online advertising requires consent. — [SCL](https://www.scl.org/ico-updates-guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/); [ICO guidance page](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies)
- Non-exempt pixels, tags and scripts must not operate before consent; withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent. — [Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/ip-technology-and-data/storage-and-access-technologies-ico-publishes-updated-guidance-on-the-use-of-cookies-and-other-tracking-technologies/); [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/uk-ico-finalizes-new-cookies-and-pixel-tracking-guidelines/)
- The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 added purpose-limited exceptions (statistical/analytics, appearance). Advertising is not among them. — [Syrenis](https://syrenis.com/resources/blog/ico-updates-guidance-on-storage-and-access-after-data-act-changes/); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/01/16/ico-consults-on-storage-and-access-cookies-guidance-102ju62?page=287)
- Status: draft Dec 2024, consultation to Mar 2025; secondary sources report the final guidance published 29 Apr 2026 (not confirmed on the ICO site in this research). — [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/uk-ico-finalizes-new-cookies-and-pixel-tracking-guidelines/); [SCL](https://scl.org/?p=16901)
- ICO Dec 2025: its cookie enforcement led to more "reject" options on top UK sites and less pre-consent cookie setting. — [ICO news Dec 2025](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2025/12/ico-action-secures-increased-cookie-compliance)
- Consent rates (not UK-specific): global average opt-in 44.5% (Usercentrics); rejection 56.0% where "Reject all" is symmetric (Cookiebot). — [Voxbooster stats roundup](https://voxbooster.com/blog/cookie-consent-banner-statistics-2026). With equally visible accept/reject buttons about 50–60% reject; without a direct reject option about 10% reject. — [Ignite](https://ignite.video/en/articles/basics/cookie-consent-studies). An older (2023) UK figure puts "Reject all" at 15–30%. — [Sourcepoint](https://sourcepoint.com/blog/uk-websites-urged-to-add-reject-all-button-what-that-means/). Survey: 48% say they accept all without reading. — [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/accept-cookies-ico-top-uk-websites-data-collection)
- Meta Conversions API (server-to-server) still requires consent; gating the pixel but not CAPI "defeats the purpose"; CAPI cannot recover events from people who refused. The old Offline Conversions API has been folded into CAPI. — [FlexyConsent](https://flexyconsent.com/blog/meta-pixel-facebook-conversions-api-consent-guide/); [ConsentStack](https://consentstack.io/blog/meta-conversions-api-consent); [Seers](https://seers.ai/blogs/facebook-ads-consent-tracking-complete-guide/)
- Landing-page views without a pixel: Meta has shown advertisers a notice that landing page views now work without a pixel, apparently via statistical modelling (practitioner report). Other sources still define LPV as pixel/CAPI-tracked, so the current rule is unclear. — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/landing-page-views-no-longer-require-a-pixel/); contradicted by [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/landing-page-views-facebook-ad-reports/)
- LPV/traffic optimisation teaches Meta to find clickers, not buyers. — [Shopify community](https://community.shopify.com/t/new-meta-pixel-with-no-purchase-data-best-optimization-strategy-for-a-new-store/650420)

### Inferences
- **If a pixel were added**, it could fire only for the roughly 40–50% of visitors who opt in (on a symmetric banner like Snickerdoodle's), so even with a pixel Meta would see only about half the sales. Adding ad trackers to a site that handles children's images also cuts against non-negotiables 1–2 and the "no behavioural ads" rule. It would be a founder stop-and-ask, and for an 18-day test it is not worth it.
- **No-pixel measurement plan (inference)**: one UTM-tagged URL per platform/ad (e.g. `?utm_source=meta&utm_campaign=xmas26`), read by first-party server logs or exempt analytics; one discount/partner code per platform (e.g. a META code, a PIN code, a TOK code), counted server-side at checkout; and a "where did you hear about us?" option at checkout. Expect codes to under-count (not everyone uses them) and UTMs to miss cross-device buys (a grandparent sees the ad on a phone and orders on a laptop). Treat the true figure as somewhere between "code orders" and "code + UTM orders + unexplained uplift during the flight".
- **Uploading offline conversions** (hashed emails of buyers) to Meta CAPI is still personal-data sharing with Meta and would need a lawful basis and probably consent. Not recommended here.

### Gaps
- No UK-specific 2025/26 measured banner acceptance rate was found.
- No primary source for the claimed 2026 German ruling on CAPI without consent.
- No official Meta page confirming no-pixel LPV optimisation in the UK.

## 4. Targeting rules: adults who are parents/grandparents, ads featuring children, and the UK ad-free subscription

### Takeaway
Age 18+ (practically 25–65+) plus UK location is always available. "Parents" demographics (by child age) still appear in Meta detailed targeting, but under Advantage+ they are only suggestions Meta can override, and detailed-targeting exclusions were removed in 2025. No grandparent-specific option was confirmed. Meta did not bring a "less personalised ads" tier to the UK; instead UK users can pay to remove ads (£2.99 web / £3.99 app per month), which slightly shrinks reach.

### Cited Findings
- Parental status remains listed in Meta detailed targeting in 2026 guides: all parents, parents of toddlers, early school age, pre-teens, teens, expecting parents (secondary sources, no primary Meta changelog found). — [Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/facebook-detailed-targeting-list-2024/); [LeadEnforce](https://leadenforce.com/blog/facebook-ads-in-2026-what-targeting-will-look-like); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-ads-targeting/)
- Under Advantage+ audience, detailed targeting inputs are "audience suggestions by default" and Meta can show ads beyond them. Advantage+ detailed targeting does not override location, age or gender. — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/targeting-suggestions/); [Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/metas-advantage-audience/)
- Detailed-targeting exclusions removed (dates vary by source: 31 Mar 2025 vs 31 Mar 2026); interest consolidation from 23 Jun 2025. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/408262/meta-further-consolidates-ad-targeting-options.html); [The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/meta-to-remove-detailed-targeting-options-in-ads-manager)
- The January 2022 removal covered sensitive topics (health, sexual orientation, religion, politics, ethnicity), not parental status. — [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/facebook-detailed-targeting)
- Meta Advertising Standards: ads must not contain content that sexually exploits or endangers children, and must comply with Community Standards. Teen targeting is restricted (no gender, no activity-based targeting of teens). — [Meta Advertising Standards (en-GB)](https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/ad-standards); [Meta child exploitation policy](https://transparency.meta.com/zh-tw/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/child-sexual-exploitation-abuse-nudity/); [VideoWeek](https://videoweek.com/?p=45919)
- UK ad-free subscription (announced Sept 2025): pay to remove ads, about £2.99/month on web and £3.99 in apps; Meta will **not** offer the EU-style "less personalised ads" option in the UK. The ICO welcomed the move. Subscribers drop out of retargeting and lookalikes, so paid reach may fall depending on uptake. No uptake numbers have been published. — [The Media Leader](https://uk.themedialeader.com/meta-launches-subscription-option-to-allow-uk-users-to-avoid-ads/); [Hallam](https://hallam.agency/blog/meta-roll-out-ad-free-subscription-option-in-the-uk/); [Trio Media](https://trio-media.co.uk/meta-ad-free-subscription-impact-for-brands/); [Impression](https://www.impressiondigital.com/blog/metas-new-ad-free-subscription/)
- TikTok: TikTok's ad minimums apply; age targeting is set per ad group. No 2026 source on an 18+-only rule was fetched (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **Compliant targeting recipe (inference)**: location UK; age 25–65+ (grandparents 50–65+ as a separate ad set if budget allows; at £300 it does not); either broad/Advantage+ or "Parents (all)" as a suggestion. Never select teen or child-related interests that could reach minors; set the minimum age to 18+ explicitly. This targets ADULTS by demographic, not children, which is consistent with the project rule. Avoid any retargeting or lookalike built from site visitors (that would need a pixel anyway).
- **Ads featuring children's art**: use the child's DRAWING and the finished product (cushion, card, print), not children's faces. Use only brand-safe or consented seed imagery, never customer children's images, in line with CLAUDE.md. This also avoids any Meta review friction around minors. Meta has no published blanket ban on non-exploitative images of children in product ads, but this was **not verified** against current policy text.
- **Ad-free subscription**: the effect on a small test is probably negligible (uptake appears low, unconfirmed). The bigger effect is that there is no UK "less personalised" tier, so ads still run on personalised inventory.

### Gaps
- Meta's current policy text on ads that depict children (not exploitative) was not retrievable. Verify in the Advertising Standards before launch.
- No confirmation that a "grandparents" demographic exists in UK Meta targeting in 2026.
- TikTok's 2026 minimum ad-targeting age in the UK (13+ vs 18+) was not verified. Set 18+ regardless.
- Boost-post vs Ads Manager: no source fetched. Common knowledge (unverified here): boosting from Instagram/Facebook is quicker but offers fewer objectives and controls. Ads Manager allows age 18+ floors, placements, UTMs per ad and lifetime budgets.

## 5. Pinterest for late-November Christmas gift intent, and creative needs for each platform

### Takeaway
Pinterest has genuine gift-search intent October–December (Pinterest says someone searches for a gift every three seconds then) and strong personalised-gift interest. Planning starts very early, though, so late-November ads catch the tail of the browse curve. Creative should be vertical (2:3 on Pinterest, 9:16 on TikTok/Reels/Stories) and show the drawing-to-product transformation.

### Cited Findings
- Pinterest: someone searches for a gift every three seconds between Oct and Dec; 58% of monthly users like personalised recommendations in the festive season. — [Pinterest Business UK](https://business.pinterest.com/en-gb/blog/festive-gifting-pinterest/)
- Searches for personalised gift ideas +72% (non-UK analysis). Holiday searches begin as early as June on Pinterest. — [Retail.org.au](https://www.retail.org.au/news-and-insights/christmas-shopping-trends-and-tips-for-businesses-according-to-pinterest); [Pinterest Business UK](https://business.pinterest.com/en-gb/blog/festive-gifting-pinterest/)
- 2020 UK IAB/Pinterest report: April Christmas searches +147% YoY; 45% of UK Pinners buy Christmas gifts for neighbours/friends. — [IAB UK PDF](https://iabuk.com/sites/default/files/public_files/Earlier-than-ever-Pinterest-Holiday-2020-UK.pdf)
- UK Ipsos analysis: UK gift searches rank fathers above mothers; example demand for "personalised gifts for couples under £50". — [Ipsos UK](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/christmas-gifting-searches-reveal-real-challenge-retailers)
- Pinterest ran a shoppable Christmas edit / 1,000 gift guides in Nov 2025. — [ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2025/11/pinterest-launches-shoppable-christmas-edit/)
- TikTok 2026 creative specs (9:16 vertical; spec guide). — [Nonsensical agency](https://nonsensical.agency/?p=5515)

### Inferences
- **Creative needed (inference, standard platform specs, not re-verified)**: Meta, a 1080×1350 (4:5) feed image or video plus a 1080×1920 (9:16) Stories/Reels version; a 6–15s video works best. Pinterest, a 1000×1500 (2:3) static Pin with a text overlay such as "turn their drawing into a cushion", or a short 9:16 video. TikTok, 9:16 video of 9–15s, native-looking (hands, a drawing, then the product reveal), with sound and captions. One hero concept, "their drawing, made real", in 2–3 variants is enough at these budgets. Use brand-safe seed drawings only.
- **Ad fatigue**: at about £20 CPM, a £1,000 Meta spend gives about 50,000 impressions. Against a UK parent/grandparent audience of millions, frequency stays low, so fatigue is unlikely at this scale. Rotating 3 creatives is enough.
- **Pinterest fit**: good for intent and cheapest per click, but cold Pinterest clicks are browsers, and attribution without the tag relies entirely on UTMs and codes. A promoted Pin's organic afterlife beyond 12 Dec is wasted this season (it helps 2027).
- **TikTok fit at tiny budgets**: weak. The Ads Manager floor (£693 for 18 days) blows a £300 test, and the audience skews younger than grandparents. Promote on an existing organic post is the only sensible TikTok option under £300, but Promote optimises for views/followers/visits with limited targeting control. Make sure age is set to 18+ if offered.
- **Confidence**: Meta cost-per-sale range, medium (backed by earlier research and the BFCM curve). Pinterest sales range, low. TikTok, low (no cost data). Consent/legal position, high on "pixel needs opt-in consent" (ICO); medium on details.

### Gaps
- No 2025/26 UK Pinterest holiday timing data. The UK figures found are from 2020 or not UK-specific.
- No official Pinterest/TikTok creative spec page fetched in this session.
