# Looking after your family's magic

Your child's drawings, their funny little photos, their name and their birthday — these are about the most precious things you could hand anyone. We don't take that lightly. So here's exactly how we look after them, in plain English. No legalese, no small print games.

## It's yours. It stays yours.

Everything you make on Snickerdoodle belongs to you. We use it to make the lovely things you order — and that's it. We never sell it. We never hand it to other families. It's your family's, full stop.

## We don't keep what we don't need.

Once we've made your order, we delete the drawing or photo you uploaded — unless you've chosen to keep it safe in your Keepsake Home. No quiet hoarding of your child's pictures on some server forever.

## No AI is learning from your child.

We use clever tools to turn a wobbly drawing into something beautiful. But the companies behind those tools are contractually forbidden from keeping your images or using them to teach any AI. Your child's drawing makes *your* product, and nothing else.

## Private is the default.

Nothing about your child is ever public unless *you* decide to share it. No public profiles, no feeds, no surprises. When you share a wishlist with Grandma, only the people with your private link can see it — and you can switch it off whenever you like.

## You're in control. Always.

See everything we hold, download it, or delete it — any time, in one tap, right from your account. It's your stuff, your rules.

## We're built for little ones from the ground up.

Snickerdoodle is for grown-ups to use, but everything about your child gets handled to a higher standard. We've designed the whole service around the UK's Children's Code, which sets out how to look after children's information properly. We didn't have to. We did it because it's your kid.

## Something not right? Tell us.

If you're ever unhappy with how we've looked after your data, tell us at **[privacy@snickerdoodle.co.uk]** — a real person will get back to you. You can always go to the regulator too, but we'd love the chance to put it right first.
