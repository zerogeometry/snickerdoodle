# Snickerdoodle — Brand & Product Foundations

*Prepared for Simon, Launch Mode Ltd · The single source of truth for how Snickerdoodle looks, sounds, feels, and behaves. Everything — the identity and the product — flows from one idea.*

---

## The one idea (read this first; everything else is downstream)

Childhood is the most creative anyone is ever going to be, and it's gone in a blink. A child draws a three-legged dog and a purple sky on a wet Tuesday, leaves it on the kitchen table, and runs off to make the next one. Most of it ends up in the recycling. **Snickerdoodle turns the throwaway magic of childhood into things a family keeps forever** — made with real craft, kept with total trust, and never, ever shared.

The signature creative tension that makes this ownable: **we frame the wonky bits in gold.** Premium craft in the service of celebrated imperfection. We don't sand off the wobbles in a child's drawing — we treat them as the most precious thing in the room and present them beautifully. That tension drives every visual and verbal choice below, and it's the thing no incumbent can copy, because corporatised scale (Moonpig) and faceless AI both instinctively tidy the wobbles away.

**Brand essence (internal north star):** *Keep the magic.*

# Part one — The brand

## Positioning statement

Snickerdoodle is the warm, private home where families turn their children's drawings, photos and milestones into beautiful keepsakes they'll treasure forever — made with craft, kept with care, and never shared. Not a card factory. Not a faceless AI gimmick. The grown-up in the room who happens to be magic with a child's scribble.

**Who we beat, and how:**
- *Against Moonpig / Funky Pigeon:* warmth and craft against gaudy, stack-'em-high, low-brow clutter.
- *Against Wonderbly:* your child's actual world (their real drawing) against a name dropped into a template.
- *Against the faceless-AI crowd:* visible, human trust against "AI generated your child."

## Brand personality

**Archetype blend:** Creator (making, imagination, self-expression) × Innocent (wonder, warmth, optimism) × a measured dose of Caregiver (safety, looking after the family). Primary register is Creator-meets-Innocent; the Caregiver is what earns trust.

**Five traits, deliberately specific:**
1. **Warm** — like the favourite aunt, not saccharine.
2. **Playful, with a wink** — the *snicker* is a giggle shared, never a snigger at a child. We adore the five-legged dog.
3. **Wonder-struck** — we genuinely think children's imagination is the best thing going.
4. **Quietly clever** — the tech is remarkable and completely invisible. We never show off.
5. **Trustworthy** — the grown-up in the room when it comes to your child's data.

## The manifesto

> Every child is an artist.
>
> They draw three-legged dogs and purple skies and self-portraits with enormous hands. They write their name backwards and mean it. They make small, strange, wonderful things — then leave them on the kitchen table and run off to make the next one.

> And because we're looking after the most precious things you have — your little one's face, their imagination, their funny little hands — we treat them that way. Everything you make here is yours. It stays yours. No funny business, no cleverness for its own sake. Just good people, looking after your family's magic.

> They're only little once.
>
> Let's keep some of it.

## Tagline

**Primary:** *Keep the magic.*

Alternates for different contexts: *Made by them. Kept forever.* · *They're only little once.* · *The fridge door, made forever.*

# Part two — Verbal identity (how we speak)

## Four voice principles

**1. Warm, like family — not a brand.**
We sound like the favourite aunt who's brilliant with the kids and great in a crisis. Never a chirpy corporate mascot, a jingle, or a "Hey there!" chatbot.

**2. Plain and human — say it how you'd say it out loud.**
No jargon, no gifting-speak, and *especially* no AI-speak. If you wouldn't say it to a tired parent at the school gate, don't write it.

**3. Playful, with a wink — laugh with, never at.**
The wonk is the whole point. We celebrate the imperfect drawing; we never patronise the child or the parent.

**4. Quietly reassuring — the grown-up in the room.**
On trust and data, say the safe thing simply and move on. No legalese, no scaremongering. Calm competence.

## Lexicon

**Words we love:** little ones · the artist (the child) · made · kept · treasure · keepsake · magic · wonky · wobbly · real · forever · yours · your family · the fridge door.

**Words we ban:** AI-generated · users · leverage · solutions · "personalised gifting solutions" · cutting-edge · disrupt · synergy. And no Moonpig-style "give a funk" over-jokeyness.

## Microcopy in the wild

| Moment | What we write |
|---|---|
| Main call to action | "Start creating" (never "Sign up") |
| At the point of upload | "This stays yours. Always. Delete it any time." |
| Empty gallery | "This is where your little one's masterpieces will live." |
| Order dispatched | "It's made, it's boxed, and it's on its way to you." |
| An error | "Something went wonky. Give it another go?" |
| Account controls | "Your stuff, your rules. See and delete anything, any time." |
| A brand signature line | "Yes, the dog has five legs. Yes, that's our favourite part." |

# Part three — Visual identity (how we look)

## Wordmark

Lowercase **snickerdoodle**, set in a warm rounded serif, in Cocoa ink on an Oat ground. Lowercase reads warm and childlike; the rounded serif reads premium-but-friendly. The double-**oo** is the ownable device — rendered as a small hand-drawn doodle moment (two little crayon circles / a sun and a swirl). A single wonky crayon star may punctuate the mark in playful contexts. Soft, warm, childlike — but crafted.

## Colour palette — the "good crayon box"

The strategic move: warm and playful, but **desaturated and premium** so it reads trustworthy and keepsake-worthy, never Crayola-cheap and never Moonpig-gaudy.

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Oat** | `#FBF5E9` | Paper / keepsake base. The breathing room. Most surfaces. |
| **Cocoa** | `#2E2723` | Warm ink. All primary text. Never pure black. |
| **Honey** | `#F2A640` | Hero. Warmth, optimism, the subtle snickerdoodle nod. CTAs, highlights. |
| **Cinnamon** | `#C8623A` | Deep warm accent. Grounds the honey; adds toasty, crafted depth. |
| **Sky** | `#84BBD0` | Calm, air, trust. |
| **Berry** | `#E8786E` | Warm coral-pink. Love, energy. |
| **Meadow** | `#93B97C` | Soft green. Play, nature. |

Core trio is Oat + Cocoa + Honey; Cinnamon deepens it; Sky / Berry / Meadow are the crayon-box accents for doodles, category coding, and moments of delight. (Optional fourth accent: Grape `#9A82B8`.)

## Typography

Recommended stack is free (Google Fonts), so you can build with it immediately:

- **Display — Fraunces** (soft optical settings). Warm, rounded, friendly-premium. Headlines, the wordmark feel, the big emotional moments. *Premium upgrade if you ever license it: Recoleta.*
- **Body / UI — Mulish** (or Hanken Grotesk). Clean humanist sans. Everything that must be trusted and read. Warmth lives in the display; clarity and trust live here.
- **The artist's hand — Caveat.** A felt-tip handwriting face, used *very* sparingly — the "artist's signature", a doodled annotation, the odd word. **Rule: one handwritten moment per view, maximum.** This is "framed in gold" expressed in type; overuse turns it into a craft-fair cliché instantly.

## Visual language & motifs

- **The doodle device** — crayon-textured squiggles, a wobbly five-point star, a sun, a heart. Hand-drawn and imperfect. Used for underlines, dividers, frames, bullets, and loading states.
- **"Framed in gold"** — the core art-direction principle: childish input, presented with crafted framing. A wonky drawing in a beautiful frame *is* the brand in one image.
- **Hero art is real children's drawings** (consented, from your own customers). This is the brand's signature texture and its single best piece of advertising — nobody else can show this.
- **Texture** — warm paper grain, crayon and pencil. Never glossy.

## Photography

Warm natural light. Real homes, real mess, real sofas and fridge doors. Documentary, not studio. The hero shot is **the reveal** — a child seeing their thing for the first time — and **before→after**: the original drawing beside the finished keepsake, so the magic transformation is visible. Anti-Moonpig gloss, anti-stock-cheese.

## Packaging & unboxing

A keepsake deserves keepsake delivery. Warm, considered, low-cost-but-thoughtful: kraft, a doodle stamp, a handwritten-style thank-you in the brand voice, and — where a drawing was posted in — the original returned safely. This is the trust promise made physical; it's also intensely content-worthy.

## What Snickerdoodle is **not** (anti-patterns)

- **Not Moonpig** — no gaudy primary clutter, no jingle, no stack-'em-high, no low-brow.
- **Not faceless AI** — never "AI-generated", no cold tech gradients, no robot mascots; the cleverness stays hidden.
- **Not Crayola-cheap** — no kindergarten primary-colour Comic Sans. We're premium-playful: the *good* crayon box.
- **Not gushy** — warm, not saccharine. Restraint with exclamation marks and emoji.

# Part four — The product, as the brand made real

The product is not separate from the brand — it's the brand you can use. Every screen below is a brand decision.

## The governing principle: time-to-magic

The thing that sells, screenshots, and spreads is a parent uploading a drawing and watching it become a beautiful real thing in seconds. That moment is the entire conversion engine, so **wow comes first; admin and cosmetics accrete later.** Anything that sits between landing and the magic is conversion bleeding away.

## First-run journey (the corrected flow)

1. **Land on Snickerdoodle's own beautiful creations.** The homepage is a gallery of *our* products — cards, prints, books — gorgeous enough that a parent thinks "I want that for mine." (These are our own range and our own margin; no public marketplace.) The feeling pulls them to **Start creating.**
2. **Straight to upload — drawing first.** "Upload your little one's drawing." The child's drawing is the hero input; a photo is offered as a secondary option. (The drawing is the defensible, viral, emotional wedge; photo-to-product is the commodity Moonpig already owns — so we lead with the drawing.)
3. **The magic, before any friction.** In seconds, the drawing appears rendered across a range of real products — the card, the print, the cushion, the book cover. *A few pictures become a whole family range in moments.* This happens **before** any account step.
4. **Trust is shown, not declared.** A single light line at the moment of upload ("This stays yours. Always."), and then trust is *demonstrated* by visible, effortless control in the UI — not a heavy "we promise!" interstitial that paradoxically raises anxiety.
5. **Only once hooked: a gentle save.** A low-friction account, framed as "save your studio" — never "build your profile". Nothing public, ever, by default.

## The Keepsake Home (your "profile", reframed)

Your profile instinct was right; the *metaphor* was wrong. "Social media page" imports audience, performance and comparison — the exact anxieties a private, safe brand must avoid. So it's not a social page. It's **the family's living keepsake home**, and it quietly does four jobs at once:

- **The trust home** — see and delete everything, in plain English, one tap. Control *shown*, not declared.
- **The studio** — where creation and generative edits happen.
- **The calendar & retention engine** — the children's names, ages and key dates live here; the reminder engine runs off them; and the home **ages with the child**. This is your 18-year relationship made literal — and your direct counter to Moonpig's reminder-engine moat.
- **The wishlist hub** — create and share gift wishlists (below).

No feed. No followers. No "who can see this?". A private keepsake box, beautifully kept.

## The Studio

Upload, edit, and generative AI styling for creations. One discipline that protects your unit economics: **cap and keep cheap any cosmetic generation** (styling a profile picture or banner) — it's a nice delight but it sells nothing, so generative spend belongs on the product previews that *do* convert, not on free cosmetics. Delight without bleeding compute.

## Catalogue & instant preview

Gallery items down one side; the product catalogue with category filters beside it; every product shows the parent's *own* uploads previewed live across the range. This is the "instant family product range" wow made persistent and browsable — the core loop they'll come back to.

## The Wishlist — elevate this; it's the keystone

This is the best mechanic in the whole product, because it turns the category's defining structural advantage — **one child pulls many wallets** (two parents, four grandparents, aunts, friends) — into a feature. A shared wishlist puts Snickerdoodle in front of a grandparent who both *buys* and *discovers the brand*. That's a viral acquisition loop and an AOV engine in one widget. Build it properly:

- **Unguessable, non-indexed, revocable share links.** A child's name and creations behind a guessable or search-indexed URL is a trust failure — design against it from day one.
- **Guest checkout.** A non-techy grandparent must be able to buy without making an account, or you lose the sale.
- **A "claimed" mechanic** so two relatives don't buy the same thing.

## Trust architecture, in the UX

- **Demonstrated, not declared.** Light one-line reassurance at upload; visible, effortless control everywhere; one-tap delete; private-by-default with nothing public unless the family explicitly chooses it.
- **Plain-English data minimisation** surfaced where it matters: what we use a photo for, and that it's gone afterwards. (Phrasing must match your actual Phase-0 data policy.)

## The "Snickerdoodle Collection" (deferred; optional)

We are **not** running an open user-to-user marketplace — it fights the trust promise, detonates the child-safety surface, and buries a solo team in moderation and IP liability. If the "some creations are too lovely to keep to one family" instinct ever returns, the only safe form is a **curated, brand-owned collection**: exceptional creations submitted opt-in, with Snickerdoodle controlling selection, IP, moderation, and the stripping of any child-identifying detail, and the creator getting a credit or royalty. Framing flips from "sell your kid's stuff to strangers" to "your creation might become a Snickerdoodle others can treasure." Prestigious, controlled, safe — and a *much* later phase, if at all. For now: our products on landing, and everything else is private creation for your own family.
